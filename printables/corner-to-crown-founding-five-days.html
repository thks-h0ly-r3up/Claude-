How to Use This Book (Read This First)

  • You are allowed to skip anything. Any exercise, any prompt, any page. Skipping is a boundary, not a failure.
  • Keep your eyes open if closing them feels unsafe. Every grounding exercise works with eyes open. Pick a different anchor if mine doesn’t work: a cold glass, a coin, the feel of your shirt.
  • You never have to write your story. Nothing in this book asks you to relive anything or tell all of it. Use initials. Use “someone.” Use a color instead of a name.
  • Protect this book. If anyone who has hurt you might read it, keep it somewhere safe, write in pencil or in code, or take photos of the pages and keep it off your phone’s main gallery. Your safety comes before your journaling.
  • If a page stirs up too much, put the pen down. Press your feet into the floor. Drink some water. Color a page. Text a safe person. If you feel unsafe, call 988 or 911.
  • If you’re using or craving, don’t wait to be “ready” for this book. Call SAMHSA’s helpline: 1-800-662-4357 (free, confidential, 24/7). Don’t stop alcohol or some other substances suddenly without medical advice. Withdrawal can be dangerous.
  • Money, housing, and transportation are real. Every soft action here is built to cost nothing. If one is out of reach, change it. I’d rather you adapt a step than quit the book.
  • Look at the tags. S E A T show you what kind of claim you’re reading. I want you to know when it’s Scripture and when it’s me.
Three lenses we’ll use

#human is what this feels like in a real body and a real life.

Introduction: Somebody Who Remembers the Floor

I’m not writing this from a pedestal. I’m writing it from the floor, and I’m still building.

I lost my sister to addiction. I’ve survived homelessness, trafficking, abuse, and years inside a destructive relationship where somebody else decided what was true. There was a stretch when I was alone, scared of people, and not sure I could trust anybody. T

When I reached for help, I sometimes found strings attached. I met people who could quote Scripture and still see somebody’s wounds as an opportunity. I got handed a watered-down verse and told to pray it away. I needed somewhere that could hold the truth about trauma without pushing God out of the room. I wish my sister had somewhere like that to reach. I can’t say it would have changed her outcome. I can say I’m building toward making that kind of help easier for somebody who’s still here.

My American Bully, Big Boy, stood beside me on some of my loneliest days. God met me while I was still trying to get up. That’s where this book comes from: a woman, her battle buddy, and a faith being lived out in the middle of rebuilding.

I’m not a counselor. I haven’t arrived. What I have is what I’ve learned, what Scripture actually teaches, and something practical you can do with a pen on a hard day.

You’ll notice I bring in some old books that not every church reads: 1 Enoch and Jubilees, which the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church counts as Scripture. I do it because they give us sharp pictures of how harm works, how limited the enemy’s reach is, and why boundaries matter. I’ll always tell you where a claim comes from, and I’ll tell you when it’s only my application. I won’t put words in God’s mouth, and I won’t tell you every hard thing is an attack. Understanding isn’t excusing. Grace isn’t enabling. Forgiveness doesn’t automatically bring back trust or access. Recovery doesn’t require pretending.

The streets taught some of us a language. I’m flipping it holy. Re-up means returning for renewal. The trap is a pattern that keeps taking more than it gives. Bando to vineyard means moving from surviving the day toward tending a life. Corner to crown means you were never only the role you survived in. It’s a picture, not a promise of instant change. A vineyard takes pruning and patience and years.

Brokenness isn’t the destination. You survived. Now we rebuild.

— Hellshaker

The Watcher Template

He came down looking like help. He left you owing.

S John 8:44; Ecclesiastes 4:9–10  ·  E 1 Enoch 6–8, 15  ·  #truthmode

Sanctuary (optional · 2 minutes)

Find three things you can see in the room. Say them quietly. Press your feet into the floor. Breathe in, then let the breath out longer than it came in. Do that twice. Keep your eyes open if that’s safer. If today is too heavy, skip to the coloring page. That counts.

God, I don’t know if it’s safe in here. Be here anyway. Help me look at this without being swallowed by it. Amen.

“He was a murderer from the beginning, and has nothing to do with the truth, because there is no truth in him. When he lies, he speaks out of his own character, for he is a liar and the father of lies.” John 8:44 (ESV) S

“Two are better than one… For if they fall, one will lift up his fellow. But woe to him who is alone when he falls and has not another to lift him up!” Ecclesiastes 4:9–10 (ESV) S

I know why you stayed. You weren’t stupid. You were loyal to the version of them that showed up first. #human

The oldest story in the Ethiopian Orthodox canon about how harm moves is 1 Enoch. E Chapters 6–8 tell of the Watchers, beings assigned to watch who instead came down by their own choice, took what was not offered, and taught things that bound people. Chapter 7 says the violence spread until it turned on humanity itself, and chapter 15 says the spirits that came out of that wreckage became evil spirits on earth. The Ethiopian church counts Enoch as Scripture, and Jude 14–15 quotes it (1 Enoch 1:9). SE I’m using it as a map of a pattern, not as a formula.

Here’s the pattern, in street words: come down looking like help, take what wasn’t offered, hand over something that binds, cut off whoever might notice, leave a mess others pay for. A Jesus says the enemy is a liar, and lying works best when it comes with access. #truthmode

Isolation is the load-bearing wall. Ecclesiastes says God built us to be picked up when we fall, so anything that removes the person who’d pick you up is doing the enemy’s work, whether it comes from a spirit or a spouse. Clinicians describe this with terms like coercive control and trauma bonding: attachment that gets stronger through cycles of harm and relief. A I’m naming behaviors, not diagnosing anybody. Only a clinician diagnoses.

Two guardrails. People are responsible for what they choose, so you don’t have to call anyone demonic to call the harm real, and “the devil made me” excuses nothing. And understanding the pattern isn’t the same as agreeing to go back to it.

Truths to stand on: Staying was survival, not stupidity. · I can name what happened without calling myself a fool. · Understanding why I stayed is not agreeing to go back.

The Leash

You are not being hunted by something with no limits.

S Job 1:12; 1 Kings 19:5–8; 1 Peter 5:8–9; James 4:7  ·  E Jubilees 10:1–13  ·  #truthmode

Sanctuary (optional · 2 minutes)

Hold something cold or textured: a glass of water, a coin, a rock. Notice its weight. Notice its temperature. Name what you’re feeling in one word, out loud or in your head. You don’t have to fix the word. Just name it.

Lord, I’m tired and I’m scared and I don’t know what’s what. Sort it with me. Amen.

“And the LORD said to Satan, ‘Behold, all that he has is in your hand. Only against him do not stretch out your hand.’ So Satan went out from the presence of the LORD.” Job 1:12 (ESV) S

“Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.” James 4:7 (ESV) S

At rock bottom, everything feels like one big attack. I remember that. And that’s exactly where you can lose the thread. #human

The Book of Jubilees, in the Ethiopian canon, has a scene after the flood: Noah’s grandchildren are being led astray, and Noah prays. The spirits are bound, but Mastema, the chief of them, pleads to keep some. The account says one tenth were left under him and the rest were bound (Jubilees 10:8–9). E The same chapter says angels taught Noah to heal with plants (10:10–13). E Job 1:12 shows the same shape in the 66-book Bible: the adversary acts only inside limits God sets. S

My application: the enemy has a leash. A That isn’t a percentage of your life. It means opposition is real and it is limited, and Peter says to be sober-minded and watchful and to resist, not to panic (1 Peter 5:8–9). S

Now look at what God does first for Elijah, who is exhausted, afraid, and wants to die. An angel doesn’t cast out anything. He says, “Arise and eat,” and Elijah sleeps, eats, and drinks before anything else (1 Kings 19:5–8). S Body first. A

So I don’t start with “who’s attacking me?” I start with the jars: body, other people, my own choices. Only after those do I ask whether something spiritual is still pressing. Not every bad night is an attack. Calling everything demonic can keep you from sleep, food, medication, and real help, and it can excuse harm people chose. It also doesn’t remove your responsibility for what you do next.

Truths to stand on: Opposition is real and it is limited. · My body is allowed to need rest. · I don’t have to figure out everything tonight.

Sort the Jars

For any hard moment, work down the jars in order. Stop at the first one that’s true and do what it says.

1

2

▶ Yes: name it honestly. Grief and safety come before spiritual language. It is not your fault.
3

▶ Yes: confess it (1 John 1:9) and choose one different move. Not shame. One move.

Legal Ground & Fence Lines

Some things in your house aren’t yours. You can finally say so.

S Ephesians 4:26–27; Ezekiel 18:20; Colossians 2:14; 2 Corinthians 2:11  ·  E Jubilees 8–10  ·  #truthmode

Sanctuary (optional · 2 minutes)

Press your palms hard against your knees, or against a wall, for ten seconds. Then let go. Notice what your body does when it lets go. That’s the feeling we’re practicing: pressing hard against something, and then setting it down.

Lord, show me what’s mine and what’s not. Give me honest eyes and gentle hands. Amen.

“Be angry and do not sin; do not let the sun go down on your anger, and give no opportunity to the devil.” Ephesians 4:26–27 (ESV) S

“The soul who sins shall die. The son shall not suffer for the iniquity of the father, nor the father suffer for the iniquity of the son.” Ezekiel 18:20 (ESV) S

There’s a difference between what was done to you and what you agreed to. Nobody told me that, and it cost me years. #human

The Greek word translated “opportunity” in Ephesians 4:27 is topos, a place or a spot. S Paul’s picture is that unresolved things can become room we leave open. The Book of Jubilees is obsessed with places: who was allotted which land, where boundaries sat, and the consequences when someone settled where he wasn’t supposed to (chapters 8–10, including 10:29–34). E I’m borrowing that language as a picture: fence lines, gates, ground. A

What kinds of ground do we leave open? Anger carried for years. Secrets we promised to keep. Inner vows like “I’ll never need anybody.” Habits we run on autopilot. Lies about ourselves we never checked. A

Now the hard part, and I won’t water it down. Exodus 20:5 says the consequences of sin can ripple to the third and fourth generation. Ezekiel 18:20 says guilt is not inherited, and a child is not condemned for a parent’s sin. S Both are true at once. Patterns travel through homes (what was modeled, feared, or kept quiet). Research on childhood stress points the same way: coping gets learned. But you aren’t sentenced. What was learned can be unlearned, with help. A

Colossians 2:14 says Christ cancelled “the record of debt that stood against us with its legal demands,” nailing it to the cross. S So this isn’t paperwork you file to earn anything. The tool on the next page is practice, a way to tell the truth and choose a different response. It doesn’t literally break a curse. A counselor or pastor can walk it with you.

Truths to stand on: What was done to me is not mine to carry. · I can end an agreement I made in the dark. · Surrender goes to God, never to someone who hurt me.

Numb Was a Teacher

It never stole the pain. It rented you a few hours away from it.

S Psalm 34:18; John 15:1–2; Galatians 5:19–21  ·  E 1 Enoch 7:1, 8:3; Jubilees 10:10–13  ·  #truthmode

Sanctuary (optional · 2 minutes)

Wrap your hands around something warm: a mug, a washcloth, your own hands. Breathe out slowly, like you’re fogging a window. Do that three times. You can stop at any point.

God, I’m not going to dress this up for You. Meet me in the real version. Amen.

“I am the true vine, and my Father is the vinedresser. Every branch of mine that does not bear fruit he takes away, and every branch that does bear fruit he prunes, that it may bear more fruit.” John 15:1–2 (ESV) S

“The LORD is near to the brokenhearted and saves the crushed in spirit.” Psalm 34:18 (ESV) S

I won’t pretend I don’t know why somebody uses. I know what it’s like to want the noise to stop. This page is not about shaming you, and relapse does not make you disposable. #human

1 Enoch 7:1 and 8:3 describe the Watchers teaching charms, enchantments, and “the cutting of roots and plants.” E In Galatians 5:19–21, Paul lists “sorcery” among the works of the flesh. The Greek word there is pharmakeia, where we get the word pharmacy. S In Paul’s world it covered drugging, charms, and enchantment together.

My application: numbing is a counterfeit of rest and comfort, a shortcut that rents you relief and sends the bill later. A That is not the same thing as medicine. Jubilees 10:10–13 has angels teaching Noah healing remedies from plants. E If a doctor prescribed it, keep taking it. Never stop prescribed medication, including medication for opioid or alcohol use disorder, because of this book. Treatment is part of the vineyard, not a lack of faith.

Addiction is also a health condition, with real changes in the body and brain, which is why “just pray harder” fails people. Prayer belongs beside treatment, not instead of it. #truthmode

Jesus says the Father prunes every branch that bears fruit (John 15:2). S Notice who gets pruned: branches still attached. A Pruning is not punishment. It’s cutting what drains, so the branch can bear. It can hurt. It does not mean you’re being thrown out.

So we won’t ask “why are you so weak?” We’ll ask what numbing was doing for you, and what else can do that job without costing you your life.

Truths to stand on: Relapse doesn’t make me disposable. · Needing relief is not a character flaw. · Pruning means I’m still attached.

The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo canon includes the Books of Meqabyan. They are not the same as the Maccabees books in other Bibles. They carry stories of believers under pressure from rulers to give in. E I’m not going to quote them here until we read them together carefully in the full edition. What I can teach today comes from the 66-book Bible, and it says the same thing.

In Daniel 3, three young men are told to bow or burn. Notice what they say: God is able to deliver, “but if not,” they still won’t bow. S Their faith doesn’t depend on the outcome. Three honest corrections. A First, refusing to bow doesn’t mean confronting someone dangerous. It can be silent. It can be a closed door and a plan. They answered the king when asked. Isolation is the enemy’s tool. Independence is you choosing safe people.
