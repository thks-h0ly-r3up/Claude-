Start Here

I did not build this on a clean stage. I built it on the floor. I know what it is to be alone and scared of people, to go looking for another plug, and to reach for help and find strings attached. I also know what it is to be handed a watered-down verse and told to pray it away. I needed somewhere that could hold the truth about trauma without pushing God out of the room. This is my attempt to build that for you.

I am not finished. I am not a counselor or a doctor, and surviving something did not make me qualified to treat you. I am building as I go, with my American Bully, Big Boy, beside me. What I can do is reach back with what I have learned, what Scripture actually teaches, and something practical you can use today.

This workbook starts today, October 7, 2026, and runs ninety-one days. Each day is one page. Each page has the same five parts, so when you are tired you will always know where to go next:

How the Calendar Works

The 364-day pattern. 1 Enoch 72–82 and Jubilees 6 describe a solar year of exactly 364 days: 52 weeks and four seasons of 91 days (13 weeks). As scholars read these texts, each season begins on the fourth day of the week, a Wednesday. Each season is three months of 30, 30, and 31 days. This workbook is one season of that pattern.

Why today is Day 1. Wednesday is the fourth day of the creation week, when God set the sun, moon, and stars (Gen. 1:14–19), and today, October 7, 2026, is a Wednesday. So Day 1 is today and Day 91 is Tuesday, January 5, 2027. I chose that anchor. I am not claiming October 7 is the official new year of the Enoch calendar. Scholars reconstruct how the 364-day year lined up with the Gregorian one in different ways, and the ancient sources do not settle it. The 364-day year is also about a day and a quarter shorter than the sun’s true year (about 365.24 days), and the old texts do not clearly say how that was corrected. Staying inside one 91-day season avoids guessing.

Every page shows three dates. The Gregorian date, the workbook’s 364-day position (Month, Day, Week), and the Ethiopian date. The Ethiopian calendar is a different solar calendar with 12 months of 30 days plus a short thirteenth month, and it is about seven to eight years behind the Gregorian one. Today, October 7, 2026, is Meskerem 27, 2019 E.C.

Genesis 1:14–19
“And God said, ‘Let there be lights in the expanse of the heavens to separate the day from the night. And let them be for signs and for seasons, and for days and years.’” (Gen. 1:14, ESV)
God’s original intent
God built time into creation as a gift: lights for signs and seasons, so people would know when to work, rest, and gather. It was a rhythm for people, never a cage.
Ethiopian canon thread
Jubilees 2 and 1 Enoch 72–82 (the Astronomical Book) tie the sun, moon, and stars to the counting of days, sabbaths, seasons, and years. 1 Enoch 72–82 describes a 364-day year of exactly 52 weeks.
Quantum picture (an analogy, not a proof)
Light does not arrive as a smooth pour. It comes in tiny packets of energy called photons. Sunlight on your skin is a countless number of them landing at once, and each one is small.
Where this picture stops: Photons describe how light behaves at very small scales. They do not prove Genesis, and Genesis is not a physics textbook. The picture only helps me notice that real things can arrive small and still carry real power.

Genesis 1:14–19
“And God said, ‘Let there be lights in the expanse of the heavens to separate the day from the night. And let them be for signs and for seasons, and for days and years.’” (Gen. 1:14, ESV)
Before there was a calendar on your phone, God hung lights in the sky and told them to keep time for us. Genesis calls them “signs,” markers that tell you where you are. Maybe you landed here tired, or a few days sober, or not sure you want to be. I remember what it is to start on the floor. A starting line does not have to be pretty. Today we plant a flag in the real date and say: this is where I am. We rebuild from here.
God’s original intent
God built time as a gift. The lights were “for signs and for seasons,” a way to know when to work, rest, and gather. They were never meant to shame you.
Quantum picture · Analogy
Sunlight arrives in tiny packets of energy called photons. Each one is small, and together they warm a whole day. Starting small is how light has always started.
Ethiopian canon thread
Jubilees 2 and 1 Enoch 72–82 also tie sun, moon, and stars to the count of days and seasons.

Blessed Before You Produce

1 · Sanctuary optional. Skip it or swap in any anchor that works for you.
Ground: Press your feet into the floor and take three slow breaths, out longer than in.   Pray: God, You are nearer than my next breath.
2 · Scripture & Teaching
Genesis 1:20–23
“And God blessed them, saying, ‘Be fruitful and multiply and fill the waters in the seas, and let birds multiply on the earth.’” (Gen. 1:22, ESV)
On the fifth day God filled the water and the sky with living things, and before they had accomplished one thing, He blessed them. Blessing came first. Some of us learned the opposite: earn it, perform, stay ready to run. A body that has lived in survival mode forgets what unguarded breath feels like. Today is not about fixing that. It is about giving your body one small, safe minute and letting it learn that breathing is allowed.
God’s original intent
God’s original order is bless, then send. Worth is given before work.
Quantum picture · Analogy
Each photon’s energy is set by its color and cannot be bargained up. Your worth is like that: set by who made you and what He says, not by your output today.
This week’s canon thread
Jubilees 2 and 1 Enoch 72–82 (the Astronomical Book) tie the sun, moon, and stars to the counting of days, sabbaths, seasons, and years.

God did not rest because He was worn out. He rested because the work was finished and good, and He gave that rest to us before any rule or failure existed. Saturday is the seventh day. God’s original intent

Quantum picture · Physics note

Ethiopian canon thread
