=== 1
canon: Jubilees 2:9 repeats the purpose of the lights: signs, sabbaths, months, years. 1 Enoch 72:1 opens the Astronomical Book with the angel Uriel showing Enoch the courses of the luminaries. The Ge’ez (Ethiopic) text is the only complete copy of 1 Enoch that survives, which is why the Ethiopian church matters so much to this story.
read: Read the sky as a clock and a witness, not a fortune. Genesis gives the lights a job: mark time. Nothing in them decides your worth or your day. If a day feels doomed, that is a feeling to bring to God, not a message from the stars.

=== 2
canon: Jubilees 2 retells the fifth day. 1 Enoch 60:7–9 names Leviathan and Behemoth as creatures God keeps in their appointed places. The canon pictures even frightening things as made and held by God.
read: A body on high alert is a creature doing what it was made to do: protect you. That alarm is not a demon and not a lack of faith. It is a protector that has been on duty too long. God blessed living things before they proved anything.

=== 3
canon: Jubilees 3:4–8 retells the making of Eve and her being brought to Adam. Sirach 6:14–17 calls a faithful friend a sturdy shelter. Sirach 12:8 adds that a friend is not known in prosperity, nor an enemy hidden in adversity.
read: Isolation often rides in on trauma and addiction. Do not call every lonely day spiritual warfare, and do not ignore how often harm works by cutting a person off. Safe people respect your no, keep your confidences, and leave you more yourself.

=== 4
canon: Jubilees 2:17–33 says the Sabbath was kept in heaven first, by the angels of the presence, before it was given to people. The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church keeps both Saturday and Sunday as holy days.
read: Rest is not a reward you earn after fixing yourself. Slavery and addiction both steal rest, and Sabbath is God giving it back. Resting on the Sabbath does not replace treatment, medication, or safety planning. It sits beside them.

=== 5
canon: 1 Enoch 48:2–3 (the Similitudes) says the Chosen One’s name was named before the sun and the stars were made. Wisdom of Solomon 7:26 calls wisdom a reflection of eternal light. Christians have long read these as pointing toward Christ, while the passages themselves are ancient Jewish writing.
read: Light came first, before sun, moon, or stars. That is why the sky is not the source of light but a lamp that points beyond itself. Jesus is the light that the dark has not overcome. You do not have to feel it for it to be true.

=== 6
canon: Jubilees 2:4 retells the second day, the firmament dividing the waters. 1 Enoch 76 describes the twelve gates of the winds, each in its ordered place.
read: God makes order by separating. That is the pattern behind a boundary. A boundary is not a curse, a grudge, or a failure to forgive. It is room. Unclear boundaries are one of the most common doors for harm, so building them is practical wisdom and not unforgiveness.

=== 7
canon: Jubilees 2:7 retells the third day, the dry land and the plants. 1 Enoch 3:1 says to observe how the trees put on their leaves and bear fruit in their season.
read: Seeds are planted in ground, not in feelings. Every recovery starts with something small and practical. The enemy of that is rarely a dramatic attack. It is usually “too big, too late, too tired,” and that deserves a practical answer.

=== 8
canon: 1 Enoch 2:1–5:3 asks the reader to watch how the heavens keep their order, and chapter 5 says the problem is people who do not. Sirach 43 is a long hymn to the sun, moon, and stars as the work of the Most High.
read: The sky does not fail, and that is not a shaming line. It is a promise that God’s order is reliable. When your own order falls apart, the reliable one is still there. A slip does not break the heavens.

=== 9
canon: Sirach 2:1–6 says to prepare for testing and that gold is tried in fire. Sirach 11:21 says it is easy in the Lord’s sight to enrich a poor person quickly and suddenly.
read: Be careful with the “testing” language. Scripture does not say God sends trauma or addiction to test you, and Sirach itself says God does not cause our wrong turns (15:11–13). What it does say is that small, faithful steps in hard times are seen. They count.

=== 10
canon: Sirach 6:7 says to gain friends through testing and not to trust them hastily. Sirach 12:10–18 warns about an enemy who speaks sweetly and weeps like a friend.
read: Discernment is not cynicism. Some people sharpen you and some use you. The canon gives you permission to test a relationship over time. Fruit tells you who is safe: respect for no, steadiness over time, and no demand for secrecy.

=== 11
canon: 1 Enoch 1:3–8 pictures the earth shaking at God’s coming, and then says that with the righteous He will make peace. That is the same pairing as Psalm 46: shaking all around and God as a refuge.
read: “Be still” is not “feel nothing.” It is “stop striving, because God is God.” When craving, grief, or panic is loud, stillness may mean sitting with a person or a phone call for fifteen minutes. That is not weak. It is how people ride out a wave.

=== 12
canon: Jubilees 4:17–19 says Enoch was the first to learn writing and to write down the signs of the sky in order. 1 Enoch 81:1–2 has Enoch told to read the heavenly tablets. Sirach 24:23 identifies wisdom with the book of the covenant.
read: Scripture is a lamp, not a weapon. It was never meant to be used to shame someone into silence. If someone handed you a verse to make your pain disappear, that was a misuse, and you are allowed to say so.

=== 13
canon: 1 Enoch 9:3–11 pictures the cries of those who have been killed and oppressed going up to the gates of heaven, and the angels bringing their case before God. Sirach 28:1–7 teaches forgiveness and warns against holding a grudge.
read: Your anger at harm is not demonic. In the canon it is heard in heaven. Forgiveness does not erase the facts, restore access, or require reconciliation. Take the anger to God and a safe person before you take it anywhere else.

=== 14
canon: Jubilees 7:1–2 says Noah planted a vineyard and waited until the fourth year for the fruit to be holy, echoing Leviticus 19:23–25. 1 Enoch 3 again points to the patience of trees.
read: Fruit takes years. Recovery and healing do too. Do not treat a slow season as a sign God left or that you are cursed. Patience is part of how the vineyard works.

=== 15
canon: Jubilees 4:29–30 says Adam died before reaching a thousand years, because a thousand years are as one day, echoing Psalm 90:4. Jubilees 23:11–12 describes the days of the generations growing shorter.
read: Every canon text that counts days is also saying they are limited. That is not meant to frighten you. It is meant to make you wise. For anyone who lost years to addiction or abuse, the numbered days are grief and invitation at once.

=== 16
canon: 1 Enoch 72 tracks the sun through six gates in the east and six in the west, with days lengthening and shortening across the year. 1 Enoch 73–74 does the same for the moon.
read: The body has its own rhythm, and trauma can scramble it. That is not a character flaw. Rebuilding sleep and daylight is spiritual work in the plain sense that a rested person can pray, think, and choose.

=== 17
canon: Sirach 15:14–17 says God created humankind and left them in the power of their own choice, and that fire and water are set before them to stretch out a hand to whichever they want.
read: This is the passage to hold when you hear “it was all written.” God sets real choices before you. Planning is not a lack of faith, and making the call is not arrogance. The Lord establishes steps that you actually take.

=== 18
canon: Jubilees 50:6–13 lists strict Sabbath prohibitions, many with severe penalties. Mark 2:27 and Luke 13 show Jesus reading the Sabbath through mercy.
read: The canon here is stricter than Jesus. That tension is worth knowing about. When a rule about rest starts hurting the person it was meant to restore, Jesus’ reading wins. Your rest rule should leave you more rested, not more ashamed.

=== 19
canon: Jubilees 1:5–7 has God tell Moses to write down all these words. 1 Enoch 82:1 has Enoch tell Methuselah to preserve the books and hand them to the generations.
read: Writing things down is an ancient act of faith. A page of stones, the moments help showed up, is a record that you can return to on a day when your mind insists nothing ever helped.

=== 20
canon: 1 Enoch 80:2–8 describes the days of the sinners as a time when the years shorten and the lights lose their order. Jubilees 6:36–38 warns of people who err about the days and feasts.
read: “The days are evil” can be read two ways: as fear, or as a call to be careful with your time. Choose the second. You do not have to name every drained hour a spiritual attack to protect your time with a boundary.

=== 21
canon: Jubilees 11:11–24 says Prince Mastema sent ravens to eat the seed, and Abram invented a plow with a seed funnel so the birds could not reach it. Sirach 7:15 says do not hate hard work, which was created by the Most High.
read: Here the answer to a spiritual threat is a practical tool. Protecting a seed took a plow. Protecting your recovery may take a calendar, a ride, and a meeting list. Practical defenses are not a lack of faith.

=== 22
canon: Sirach 17:1–4 says the Lord created humans out of earth. Wisdom of Solomon 2:23 says God created humans for incorruption and made them in the image of His own eternity.
read: Dust and image, both. Nothing in the canon says addiction or abuse makes a person worth less. Shame tells you that you are only dust. God’s word says you are dust and breath, and that He remembers it.

=== 23
canon: Wisdom of Solomon 7:1–6 has Solomon say he is mortal like everyone else, born the same way as every person. Sirach 38:1–14 honors the physician as someone the Lord created.
read: The body is not the enemy. Caring for it is stewardship, including medical and clinical care. Prayer and treatment are not rivals.

=== 24
canon: Sirach 7:15 says not to hate hard labor, which the Most High created. Jubilees 3 retells Adam being placed in the garden to tend and keep it.
read: Work before the fall was not a curse. If work has only meant exploitation for you, that was harm, not God’s design. A practical step toward honest work can be spiritual in its own right.

=== 25
canon: Sirach 31:1–2 says wakefulness over wealth wastes the flesh. Sirach 40:5–7 describes troubled thoughts and fears that confuse a person’s sleep at night.
read: Fear that steals sleep is described in the canon without blame. If your sleep is broken by trauma, that is not weak faith and not always spiritual attack. Rest where you can, and ask for medical help if it will not come.

=== 26
canon: Wisdom of Solomon 15:11 says a person fails to know the One who formed him and breathed a living spirit into him. Job 33:4 says the same: the breath of the Almighty gives me life.
read: Every breath is a gift you did not make. That is not meant to shame you for not feeling grateful. It is permission to start from what is true: you are alive, and God is the reason.

=== 27
canon: Sirach 2:11 says the Lord is compassionate and merciful, forgives sins, and saves in time of distress. Wisdom of Solomon 11:23–12:2 says God overlooks sins so that people may repent, and spares all things because they are His.
read: Shame and guilt are different. The canon speaks of mercy toward the person while still calling wrong, wrong. You can take responsibility and still refuse the verdict that you are worthless.

=== 28
canon: Sirach 51:1–12 is Ben Sira’s prayer of thanks for being rescued from death, from the depths of Hades, and from slander. 1 Enoch 22 pictures hollow places where the dead wait for judgment.
read: DOA is not your end. God pulls people out of pits. Whatever the dead wait for, the living still have days, and you are among them. Take the ground check as a gift, not an accusation.

=== 29
canon: 1 Enoch 98:4 says sin was not sent on the earth but created by people themselves. 1 Enoch 6–16 tells of Watchers who corrupted the earth from outside. Sirach 15:11–13 says do not blame the Lord for your failing.
read: The canon holds both: people choose, and evil also comes at people from outside. Both are true of addiction. You are responsible for your next step, and you did not start the harm that shaped you. Hold both without drowning in either.

=== 30
canon: Wisdom of Solomon 9:15 says a perishable body weighs down the soul and this earthly tent burdens the thoughtful mind. Sirach 2 and 4 speak of being tested and tried.
read: A clay jar under pressure is the right picture. The canon admits the weight. You do not have to pretend it is light. Naming where you feel pressure in your body is honest prayer.

=== 31
canon: Sirach 17:24–26 says God gives a return to those who repent and tells them to turn back to the Lord. Sirach 5:5–7 warns against presuming on mercy by adding sin to sin.
read: Both sides matter. Relapse is not the last word, and it is not nothing either. The canon holds grace and responsibility together. Make the plan, call the person, and do not hide.

=== 32
canon: Sirach 51:23–27 invites the untaught to take up wisdom’s yoke and says, “I have labored little and found much rest,” which scholars often set beside Matthew 11:28–30. Sirach 6:24–31 describes the yoke of wisdom as something that becomes a robe of gladness.
read: Jesus’ invitation has an echo in the canon, and it is rest for the weary. A divided heart is welcome. You do not have to fix the war inside before you come to Him.

=== 33
canon: Sirach 1:28 says not to approach the Lord with a divided mind. 1 Enoch 91:4 says to love uprightness and not to approach it with a double heart.
read: A divided heart is common, and the canon names it plainly. The answer is not self-hatred. It is to ask God to unite you, which is what Psalm 86:11 does.

=== 34
canon: Sirach 15:11–20 says not to say “the Lord caused me to fall away,” for He does not do what He hates, and that He commanded no one to be wicked.
read: This one matters a lot for how we read struggle. God does not cause your relapse, and a “spiritual conflict” label is not an excuse. Both mercy and responsibility belong here, and neither cancels the other.

=== 35
canon: Sirach 22:27–23:6 is a prayer that God would set a guard over the mouth and a seal on the lips, and keep the heart from being led by passion.
read: A prayer for a guard is the same as an if-then plan in older words. Planning ahead is not a lack of trust. It is how you guard the wellspring while you are clear.

=== 36
canon: Sirach 6:14–17 calls a faithful friend a sturdy shelter and a treasure. Sirach 12:8–9 says a friend is not known in prosperity, and an enemy is not hidden in adversity.
read: Adversity shows who stays. If someone disappeared when you were at your lowest, that is information about them, not a verdict on you. The circle you draw today can be small and still be real.

=== 37
canon: Sirach 4:1–10 tells the reader not to deprive the poor, to help the oppressed, and to be a father to orphans and a husband to widows. 4:9 says to deliver the oppressed from the hand of the oppressor.
read: Burden-bearing is a command in the canon too. It is not a favor to the weak. If you need help, you are not a burden. If you can help, you are not above it.

=== 38
canon: Sirach 37:1–6 says some friends are friends in name only, and warns about a companion who turns into an enemy in time of trouble.
read: A faithful friend is a gift and a rare one. Do not call every betrayal a spiritual attack, and do not blame yourself for trusting. Grow in discernment, and keep the door open for the ones who stay.

=== 39
canon: Sirach 7:34 says do not fail those who weep, but mourn with those who mourn. Wisdom of Solomon 3:1–3 says the souls of the righteous are in God’s hand and are at peace, though to the foolish they seemed to have died.
read: Presence is a ministry. If someone you love is gone, the best companions say little. Wisdom 3 is a hope the canon offers, and it is a hope, not a verdict on any one person.

=== 40
canon: Sirach 6:34–37 tells the reader to stand in the assembly of the elders and to meditate on the commandments. 1 Enoch 1:1 begins with “the words of the blessing of Enoch.”
read: Gathering was always part of the faith. If a gathering hurt you, you are allowed to leave and look again. Safe community does not charge for belonging, demand secrets, or punish questions.

=== 41
canon: Sirach 12:10–18 warns that an enemy may speak sweetly and weep like a friend, and that if he finds an opening he may not be satisfied until he has drawn your blood.
read: Isolation can be chosen, imposed, or both, and the canon does not mock someone for being careful. Distance from a dangerous person is not the isolation this proverb warns about.

=== 42
canon: Sirach 40:23–24 says kindred and helpers are for a time of trouble. Ecclesiastes 4:12 says a threefold cord is not quickly broken.
read: Build your support in more than one strand. One person cannot be your whole circle, and a hotline number counts as a strand.

=== 43
canon: Jubilees 48:9–19 retells the Exodus with Prince Mastema trying to stop Moses and then being bound while Israel goes out. 1 Enoch 10:4–8 has the angel Raphael bind Azazel and cover him in darkness.
read: In these texts, the barrier falls because God acts, not because a person was stronger. That does not mean you wait passively. It means you take the smallest crack and ask for help with it. A wall that “can’t be climbed” may still have a door.

=== 44
canon: 1 Enoch 94–99 (the Epistle of Enoch) pronounces woes on oppressors and offers hope to those who suffered. 96:1–3 says the righteous who have suffered should be of good hope.
read: God is against chains, and the canon does not shy away from saying so. If you were bound, the injustice is real and heaven knows it. Releasing tension in the body is a small way to practice what freedom feels like.

=== 45
canon: 1 Enoch 51:1–2 says the earth will give back what was entrusted to it and Sheol will give back what it received. 1 Enoch 91:10 says the righteous will arise from sleep.
read: Resurrection hope runs through the canon, and Jesus raising Lazarus shows it. But notice who did the unbinding: the community. You can be alive and still wrapped, and asking for help with the wrappings is not weakness.

=== 46
canon: Jubilees 50:6–13 lists strict Sabbath prohibitions. Jubilees 10:10–13 says angels taught Noah remedies from the plants of the earth for diseases. Luke 13 has Jesus healing on the Sabbath.
read: The canon contains both strictness and healing. Jesus reads the Sabbath as a day for loosing people. The passage names a spiritual cause for this woman’s bent back, but it does not say every ailment has one, and neither do I.

=== 47
canon: Jubilees 50:1–5 speaks of the jubilee years. Jubilees 1:22–25 promises that God will circumcise their hearts and create in them a holy spirit.
read: Liberty is built into the calendar of the canon. Jesus announces it in Nazareth as present. Where release feels far away, name what you want freedom from and for, and take one concrete step toward it.

=== 48
canon: Jubilees 1:19–21 has Moses pray that the spirit of Belial not rule over Israel and that God create in them a clean heart.
read: This prayer sits beside Psalm 51:10 and Galatians 5:1. It asks for freedom and a clean heart in the same breath. The canon’s answer to bondage is prayer plus a changed heart, and it does not skip the practical steps that make standing firm possible.

=== 49
canon: Baruch 4:21–29 tells the children to take courage because the One who brought the calamity will bring everlasting joy. Jubilees 1:15–18 promises that God will return them and build His sanctuary among them.
read: These texts attribute exile to God’s discipline of a people. Do not turn that into “God did this to you.” Most trauma is human harm, and God is against it. The part to keep is the promise: a valley of trouble can become a door of hope.

=== 50
canon: Wisdom of Solomon 9:13–18 asks who can learn the counsel of God and says our plans are unstable. 1 Enoch 93:11–14 asks who can hear the voice of the Holy One without being troubled.
read: Not knowing is the human condition. No soul contract or hidden script has to be decoded for you to live faithfully. The canon’s answer to not knowing is trust and the next faithful step.

=== 51
canon: Sirach 50:22–24 calls the reader to bless the God who works wonders everywhere. Sirach 38:16–23 says to weep bitterly for the dead and mourn, and then to be comforted.
read: Gratitude and grief are not enemies. The canon tells people to mourn well and then to receive comfort, in that order. Do not hurry grief, and do not let it be the only thing you are allowed to feel.

=== 52
canon: Wisdom of Solomon 16:20–29 retells the manna as the food of angels. 16:28 says people must rise before the sun to give thanks and pray at the dawning of the light.
read: Daily bread and the dawn are tied together in this text. It is one more reason to treat a small morning anchor as holy. Needing the day’s bread and not the month’s is how God taught people to trust.

=== 53
canon: Sirach 30:21–25 says not to give yourself over to sorrow, and that anxiety brings premature old age. 30:23 says to delight your soul and comfort your heart.
read: This is a practical word about worry in a canon text. It does not shame the anxious. It says to comfort your heart. A worry window is a way of doing that.

=== 54
canon: 1 Enoch 48:4 says the Chosen One will be a light to the nations and the hope of those who are troubled of heart.
read: Advent is waiting in the dark without pretending it is morning. If you are in that place, you are in the part of the story where waiting is faithful. Light a small light and say what you are waiting for.

=== 55
canon: Sirach 23:9–11 warns against accustoming the mouth to oaths. Sirach 5:10–14 says to be steady in your convictions and quick to hear but deliberate in answering.
read: A plain no is not rudeness. It is integrity. If a gathering is unsafe, you do not owe a long explanation or your presence.

=== 56
canon: Sirach 11:21–28 warns that no one knows the future and that a day of prosperity can be forgotten in adversity. Jubilees 11:23–24 has Abram sowing in faith.
read: The canon admits that the future is not yours to see. It never says to stop sowing because of that. Do one small thing without a guarantee.

=== 57
canon: Baruch 3:34–35 says the stars shine at their watches and answer when called. 1 Enoch 48:3 says a name was named before the sun and stars were made.
read: Names matter in the canon. A name given in harm is not final, and God’s name for you stands. If a label has followed you, you are allowed to put it down.

=== 58
canon: 1 Enoch 43:1–4 says Enoch saw how God called the stars by their names and they obeyed Him. Baruch 3:34–35 has the stars answer, “Here we are.”
read: This is the closest the canon comes to your sky tonight. Stars are called by name, and so are you. The sky is not a message about your fate. It is a picture of a God who knows each one.

=== 59
canon: Jubilees 14 retells the story of Hagar and Ishmael. 1 Enoch 9:10 says the cry of those who perished has gone up to the gate of heaven.
read: God sees the trafficked, the used, and the run-away. The canon does not minimize their cries. If you or someone you know is in danger, call for help now: that is also a way of agreeing with a God who hears.

=== 60
canon: 1 Enoch 9:5 says all things are naked and open in God’s sight and nothing can hide from Him. Sirach 42:18–20 says He searches the abyss and the human heart.
read: Being known by God is not surveillance. In the canon it is how the oppressed are heard. For anyone who was watched by someone who meant harm, this is a different kind of seeing.

=== 61
canon: Sirach 18:8–14 says God is patient with people and pours out mercy on them. Baruch 2:31 says God will give them a heart that obeys.
read: Mercy is renewed, and so are chances to begin again. A new month does not erase anything, and it does not need to. It is permission to start the next day from where you are.

=== 62
canon: I do not know of a direct canon parallel for the white stone in Revelation 2:17. The nearest thread is names: 1 Enoch 43:1–4 and Baruch 3:34–35 have the stars called by name.
read: A new name is promised by Jesus to those who overcome. Names assigned by shame are not final. You can refuse a label honestly and still wait for the name you have not yet received.

=== 63
canon: Wisdom of Solomon 5:15–16 says the righteous will receive a glorious crown and a beautiful diadem from the Lord’s hand. Sirach 6:30–31 describes wisdom’s fetters as a golden ornament and a crown of gladness.
read: A crown is a gift for the one who mourned. Dignity is not earned by being fixed. A dignity act, however small, is a way of agreeing with what God says.

=== 64
canon: Jubilees 49 retells the Passover in Egypt. Jubilees 50 closes the book on the Sabbath and jubilee years.
read: Deliverance and rest are tied together in the canon. If rest felt unsafe because it was punished, your body may need time to believe the change. Rest is evidence that you are no longer owned.

=== 65
canon: Sirach 30:14–16 says good health is better than gold. Sirach 30:23 says to delight your soul and comfort your heart.
read: Elijah was fed and slept before he heard God. Do not make a spiritual project of what is a physical need. Eat, drink, sleep, and then listen.

=== 66
canon: Sirach 38:1–14 says to honor the physician and to pray, and also to let the physician do his work. Jubilees 10:10–13 says angels taught Noah remedies from plants. 1 Enoch 7:1 and 8:3 say the Watchers taught forbidden “cutting of roots” and enchantments.
read: The canon is not uniform here. Enoch fears corrupted knowledge, and Jubilees and Sirach bless medicine. Read with Jesus: He called Himself a physician for the sick (Mark 2:17). Treatment is not witchcraft. Be careful of anyone who sells spiritual cures for addiction or illness and tells you to drop real care.

=== 67
canon: Sirach 24:7–11 has wisdom seek a resting place and find it in Jacob. Jubilees 2:17–33 treats the Sabbath as a heavenly sign.
read: Hebrews says a Sabbath rest remains. That means rest is promised and still on its way. In the meantime you can practice it, in pieces, with your whole body.

=== 68
canon: Wisdom of Solomon 17:1–21 describes terror in the darkness, and 17:12 says fear is the surrender of the help that reason offers.
read: The canon describes night terror honestly. Fear that makes reason go quiet is not unbelief. If your home is not safe, call for help. If your nights are unsafe inside you, say so to a doctor or counselor too.

=== 69
canon: 1 Enoch 12:1–2 says Enoch was hidden and no one knew where he was. In 1 Enoch 13 he then intercedes on others’ behalf.
read: Withdrawing to pray and then going back to the people is the pattern here, and it is Jesus’ pattern in Mark 1. Saying no to good things is how you say yes to the right ones.

=== 70
canon: Sirach 6:18–19 says to come to wisdom like one who plows and sows. Jubilees 11:23 has Abram invent a seed plow.
read: Clearing thorns takes tools and help. If the thorn is a substance, bring a supporter before you start pulling.

=== 71
canon: 1 Enoch 38:4 says the light of the Lord of Spirits will be seen on the face of the holy. Wisdom of Solomon 7:26 calls wisdom a reflection of eternal light.
read: Jesus is the light of the world. Following Him means walking in what He shows. A gap between what you say and do is information. It is not an attack, and it is not the end of you.

=== 72
canon: 1 Enoch 108:11–15 says God will bring those who loved His name into shining light. 108:8–9 says they were tried in fire.
read: Being light in the Lord is an identity before it is a behavior. The canon admits the testing. It does not say the testing was good, only that those who endured will shine.

=== 73
canon: Sirach 50:5–7 describes the high priest Simon as the morning star among the clouds, like the moon when full, like the sun shining on the temple. Sirach 43:1–12 is a hymn to the sun, moon, and stars.
read: Light is a gift, and a quiet good deed is a way to hold it. The canon praises the lights of heaven as the work of God, which is a different thing from being led by them.

=== 74
canon: Sirach 43:1–5 calls the sun a marvelous instrument, the work of the Most High, and says that at noon it parches the land. 1 Enoch 72 follows the sun through its gates.
read: God does not change like the shadows do. Sit in the sun and receive a good gift. You are not earning anything today.

=== 75
canon: Sirach 24:19–22 says those who eat wisdom will hunger for more and those who drink will thirst for more. Sirach 15:3 says she will feed him with the bread of understanding and give him the water of wisdom to drink.
read: My own word for God, the OG Plug, is a metaphor and not a title from Scripture. What the old source promised and took is a real comparison. The Source gives without taking more than it gives.

=== 76
canon: 1 Enoch 72 describes the longest day as two-thirds of the day and the shortest night as one-third, which is steeper than the real daylight at Jerusalem. The chapter is a schematic and not a measurement.
read: Today is the winter solstice, the longest night in the Northern Hemisphere. After today the days lengthen. That is astronomy, not a sign about you. Let the longest night be honest, and look for the small sign that light returns.

=== 77
canon: Sirach 17:15–20 says people’s ways are always before the Lord and are not hidden from His eyes. Sirach 17:24–26 invites them to return.
read: Bringing something to the light is not exposure. It is telling someone safe. Hidden things lose power when named to a trustworthy person.

=== 78
canon: Sirach 24:17–21 has wisdom say “Like the vine I bud forth delights.” 1 Enoch 10:18–19 pictures a future in which the vine will be planted and bear abundant fruit. Jubilees 7:1 says Noah planted a vineyard.
read: Fruit grows from connection, not from straining. The canon sees vineyards as God’s and as ours to tend. Abiding is not passive. It is staying close to the Source.

=== 79
canon: Wisdom of Solomon 18:14–15 says that while gentle silence enveloped all things and night was in the midst of its swift course, God’s all-powerful word leaped from heaven.
read: Christmas Eve is heavy for many. This line suits a night like that. It says that God arrives in the middle of the night, in silence. If tonight is hard, you are not behind.

=== 80
canon: Sirach 24:8–12 has wisdom say that her Creator assigned a place for her tent, and she took root in a people. John 1:14 uses a word for dwelling that means pitching a tent.
read: The Word became flesh and moved in. That is the heart of it: God came near. If today is hard, that is not a failure of Christmas. Let one small thing be a gift.

=== 81
canon: Wisdom of Solomon 4:7–14 says the righteous, though they die early, will be at rest, and 4:13 says that being perfected in a short time, he fulfilled long years. Wisdom 3:1–3 says the souls of the righteous are in the hand of God.
read: This is the closest the canon comes to the language of a short life being “fulfilled.” It is about the righteous, it is a hope and not a formula, and it does not say a death was planned, required, or agreed to. See the essay on soul contracts for how to hold this carefully.

=== 82
canon: Jubilees 7 retells Noah’s vineyard, his wine, and the first fruits. 1 Enoch 10:19 pictures abundant vines. Sirach 6:19 describes wisdom like one who plows and sows.
read: Be careful with the vineyard image. Noah’s vineyard also led to Noah’s fall in Genesis 9:20–21. Good things can be misused, and tending a vineyard includes tending what you do with its fruit.

=== 83
canon: Sirach 19:1–2 says that workers who are drunkards will not become rich, that whoever despises small things will fall little by little, and that wine and women lead the intelligent astray.
read: Little foxes are small things, and the canon says to take small things seriously. This is not shaming anyone. It is a reason to put a fence around the small ones before they grow.

=== 84
canon: Sirach 4:17–19 says wisdom first walks with a person on winding paths and tests them, then comes straight back and gladdens them. Wisdom of Solomon 3:5–6 says they were tested like gold in a furnace.
read: Be careful here too. The canon uses refining language, and it is not saying that abuse or trauma is God’s pruning. A vinedresser prunes for fruit with care. If what happened to you was harm, call it harm.

=== 85
canon: Jubilees 5:13–18 pictures judgments written on the heavenly tablets, and also says there is forgiveness written for those who turn. 1 Enoch 81:1–2 has Enoch told to read the heavenly tablets. Sirach 15:14–17 says God left humankind to their own choice.
read: This is the best place to see why “it was all written” is not the whole canon. Even the tablets include a pardon for those who turn. Choice is real. Choose life, and do not choose alone.

=== 86
canon: 1 Enoch 104:1–6 tells the righteous to be of good hope and says they will shine like the lights of heaven. Daniel 12:3 says the wise will shine like the brightness of the sky.
read: Shining like the sky is a promise about the end, not a description of this week. On New Year’s Eve, a kind look back and a safe night are enough.

=== 87
canon: 1 Enoch 72:1 speaks of the courses of the luminaries until the new creation is accomplished, which lasts forever. Jubilees 1:29 looks ahead to the day of the new creation.
read: Renewal is the canon’s horizon. Revelation 21:5 says God is making things new. It does not say the year will be perfect. Begin with small practices.

=== 88
canon: 1 Enoch 100:5 says God will set a guard of holy angels over all the righteous. Jubilees 10 describes angels binding evil spirits at Noah’s prayer.
read: The canon speaks of guarding. Psalm 121 does too. Both can be held beside real safety steps. A guard in prayer does not replace locking a door, a safety plan, or a phone number.

=== 89
canon: Baruch 4:1 says wisdom is the book of the commandments of God and that all who hold her fast will live. Sirach 15:14–17 says choice is set before each person.
read: “As for me” is a first-person choice, and you cannot make it for anyone else. Not your family, not your friends, and not someone you lost.

=== 90
canon: 1 Enoch 91:4 says to love uprightness and walk in it, and not to approach it with a double heart. 1 Enoch 91:18–19 describes the path of righteousness and the path of violence.
read: A no-list and a yes-list are a way of pondering the path of your feet. Keep them short and share them with a safe person.

=== 91
canon: Sirach 50:20–21 describes Simon the high priest coming down to bless the whole congregation. 1 Enoch 1:1 opens with “the words of the blessing of Enoch.”
read: This blessing asks for keeping, light, grace, and peace. Receive it as a gift and not as a prediction of how your life will go. The next season begins when you decide it does.
