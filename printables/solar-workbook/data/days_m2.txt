q: “For now we see in a mirror dimly, but then face to face. Now I know in part; then I shall know fully, even as I have been fully known.” (1 Cor. 13:12, ESV)
teach: Paul is not saying you cannot know anything. He is saying you know in part, and one day fully, and meanwhile you are already fully known. That is a kindness. This week has holidays in it, and that can mean grief, family stress, old using times, or an empty chair. You do not have to know how it will go. Today we make a holiday plan: what you know, what you do not, and what you choose to trust.
intent: God asks us to trust Him with what we cannot see, because we are already fully known by Him.
quantum: Analogy: Heisenberg’s uncertainty principle says you cannot know a particle’s exact position and exact momentum at once. It is built into nature, not a flaw in our tools. We can still make precise predictions about what is likely.
canon: Wisdom of Solomon 9:13–14 asks who can learn the counsel of God and says our plans are unstable.
r1: What do I know for sure about this week?
r2: What do I not know, and which part of that scares me most?
r3: What am I choosing to trust God with?
action: Make three columns: “What I know,” “What I do not know,” “What I choose to trust.” Add a holiday plan: a way out, a ride, a time limit, and one person to call.
s1: Fill in the three columns.
s2: Write a holiday exit plan: how you will leave if you need to.
s3: Write one number to call if the day gets hard, such as 988 or a sponsor.
prayer: God, I do not know how this season will go, and I am scared of parts of it. You know me fully. Be with me where I cannot see, and give me a way out when I need one. Amen.
decl: I do not have to know everything. I am fully known.
cal: Holiday plan

intent: Jesus invites us to live in today, and does not shame anxious hearts.
