About the Ethiopian canon texts. 1 Enoch (Henok) and Jubilees (Kufale) survive in full only in Ge’ez, the classical language of Ethiopia. The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church receives them, along with Sirach, Wisdom of Solomon, and Baruch, as canon. Most other churches do not treat them all as Scripture, though Jude 14–15 quotes 1 Enoch 1:9. I treat them as ancient witnesses worth knowing, and I let the Bible carry the weight. Where a verse has no real parallel in the canon, I say so instead of inventing one.

How “exact” the sky is. Each day’s moon, sun, and star data is computed for the evening of that date at 02:00 UTC the next day, which is about 9 p.m. Central, 10 p.m. Eastern, and 7 p.m. Pacific. Moon illumination and age are accurate to about a percent or better, and constellations use the modern IAU boundaries. Sunrise, sunset, and moonrise depend on where you stand, so they appear only if a location is added. Meteor-shower dates are annual and approximate.

How to Read This Book

Signs get abused, so this book uses a short set of tests. Before you decide what any verse, canon text, dream, or sky event “means,” run it through these six. If it fails one, set it down.

#TestThe question
1ChristDoes it point toward Jesus, and not toward me, a sign, a person, or a product?
2ScriptureDoes it agree with the clear teaching of the Bible read in context? Canon texts outside the Protestant Bible can add color but cannot override it.
3FruitDoes it lead toward love, truth, safety, humility, and responsibility, or toward fear, secrecy, shame, and control?
4FreedomDoes it leave room to choose, to say no, to ask questions, to leave, and to get treatment?
5CostDoes it ask for money, silence, or loyalty to a leader in exchange for the “answer”?
6HumilityDoes it admit what it does not know, including about the dead?

What the Bible says about reading the sky

  • The lights are for time. “Let them be for signs and for seasons, and for days and years” (Gen. 1:14, ESV). A sign marks something. It does not decide it.
  • The sky tells about God. “The heavens declare the glory of God” (Ps. 19:1, ESV). “For his invisible attributes … have been clearly perceived, ever since the creation of the world, in the things that have been made” (Rom. 1:20, ESV).
  • Do not worship them. Deuteronomy 4:19 warns against being drawn to bow down to the sun, moon, and stars. Jeremiah 10:2 says not to be dismayed at the signs of the heavens as the nations are. Isaiah 47:13–14 mocks those who gaze at the stars to tell the future.
  • They are praising Him. “Praise him, sun and moon, praise him, all you shining stars!” (Ps. 148:3, ESV). He “determines the number of the stars; he gives to all of them their names” (Ps. 147:4, ESV).

So this book will say, “Here is the sky tonight, and here is what the canon and Scripture say about the verse for the day.” It will not say, “This moon means a hard week for you.” If anyone tells you a star position decides your relationships, your sobriety, or your safety, that is a claim Scripture does not make.

Reading the Sky Chips and the Moon

Reading the sky chips on each day

  • Moon: phase, how much of the visible face is lit, days since the new moon, and the constellation it stands in.
  • Sun: the constellation behind it and its declination (how far north or south of the celestial equator, in degrees).
  • Star: a bright star near due south around 9 p.m. local sun time, so you can find it. It is a landmark, not a message.
  • Enoch: the moon’s light counted in fourteenths, the old way (see below).

A note on the moon and the calendar

The Bible itself speaks of new moons and festivals (Num. 28:11; Ps. 81:3). The Enoch and Jubilees tradition, which this workbook follows for its 364-day count, argues for reckoning by the sun and warns against dating the festivals by the moon (Jubilees 6:36–37). Both appear in this canon. I show the moon because it is beautiful and real and because you asked for it, and I count the days by the sun.

1 Enoch also counts the moon’s light in fourteenths (chapters 73–74 and 78), growing for about two weeks and then fading for two. The “Enoch’s count” chip on each day is that old way of saying the same thing, and it is approximate.

Trauma in the Canon

The canon does not hand trauma a tidy explanation. Read together, it says a few plain things.

1. The cry is heard

1 Enoch 9:3–11 pictures the souls of the killed and the groans of the oppressed going up to the gates of heaven, and the angels bringing the case before God. That is a canon witness that suffering inflicted on people is not invisible to heaven. Compare Exodus 3:7 and Genesis 4:10.

2. Fear is described without blame

Wisdom of Solomon 17 describes terror in the dark, and 17:12 says fear is the surrender of the help that reason offers. Sirach 40:5–7 describes troubled thoughts that disturb sleep. Neither blames the sufferer. They treat a frightened body and mind as something real that needs care.

3. “Tested like gold” needs a warning label

Sirach 2:1–6 and Wisdom 3:5–6 use refining language. In their setting it is about perseverance in faith. It is not a claim that God sends abuse, assault, or trafficking to refine a person. Job’s friends tried that reading and God rebuked them for it (Job 42:7). Human harm is human harm.

4. Mourning is allowed

Sirach 7:34 says to mourn with those who mourn. Sirach 38:16–23 says to weep for the dead and then to receive comfort and not be consumed. Both halves matter.

5. How this book reads it

  • A trauma response is not evidence of weak faith or a demon.
  • Grounding, sleep, safety, and counseling are part of rebuilding, not substitutes for faith.
  • Nothing in these pages asks you to relive anything, name the harm, or forgive on a timeline.
If remembering floods you, stop, look around the room, name five things you can see, and put the book down. A page is never worth your safety.

Addiction and Bondage in the Canon

Addiction as we name it today is not a category in these texts, but the canon knows what it is to be mastered by something.

What it says about wine and bondage

  • Sirach 31:25–30 says not to try to prove yourself by wine, because wine has destroyed many. Sirach 19:1–2 says workers who are drunkards will not become rich, and that whoever despises small things will fall little by little.
  • Proverbs 23:29–35 describes the wounds and the pull of drink with a hard honesty.
  • Noah’s vineyard (Gen. 9:20–21; Jubilees 7) is a vineyard that bore fruit and then led to his fall. The same good thing can be misused. This is told without disgust and it does not end the story of Noah.

What it says about getting out

  • Prayer for freedom and a clean heart. In Jubilees 1:19–21 Moses prays that the spirit of Belial not rule over Israel and that God create a clean heart in them, much like Psalm 51:10.
  • Return is open. Sirach 17:24–26 says God gives a return to those who repent. Proverbs 24:16 says the righteous fall seven times and rise again.
  • Responsibility is real. Sirach 5:5–7 warns against presuming on mercy. Sirach 15:11–20 says not to blame the Lord for your failing. God does not cause a relapse.
  • Medicine is allowed. Sirach 38:1–14 says to honor the physician and to pray. Jubilees 10:10–13 says angels taught Noah remedies from plants. Jesus calls Himself a physician for the sick (Mark 2:17).

How this book reads it

  • Grace is not enabling, and understanding is not excusing. Hold both.
  • A relapse does not make a person disposable. It does call for a plan and for people.
  • Prayer does not replace treatment, detox with medical support, medication, or a recovery community.
  • Be careful of any teaching that sells a spiritual cure and tells you to drop real care.
Withdrawal from alcohol and some other substances can be medically dangerous. Do not stop suddenly without a clinician. SAMHSA 1-800-662-4357 can help find treatment. In an emergency call 911.

Spiritual Warfare, Carefully

The Enoch and Jubilees tradition has a much larger cast of spiritual beings than most Bibles do. It is worth knowing what these books say, and being careful about what they do not say.

What the texts say

  • 1 Enoch 6–16. The Watchers, angels who descended and taught people forbidden things and fathered the giants. Chapter 10 has the angel Raphael bind Azazel and speaks of healing the earth. 15:8–12 says spirits came from the giants. Jude 6 and 2 Peter 2:4 both mention angels who sinned.
  • Jubilees 10. After the flood Noah prays, evil spirits are bound, a tenth is left under Prince Mastema, and the angels teach Noah remedies from plants.
  • Jubilees 11, 17, 48. Mastema sends ravens to eat the seed, urges the testing of Abraham, and opposes Moses at the Exodus. In each, God acts and the opposition fails.

What they do not say

None of these books says every trauma response, illness, addiction, intrusive thought, or difficult person is demonic. None says spiritual warfare removes responsibility. And none says an exercise, a ritual, or a worksheet literally breaks a curse. Broken chains are a symbol of freedom, and freedom also takes people, time, and help.

What the Bible adds

  • Ephesians 6:10–18 says our struggle is not only against flesh and blood, and then spends its words on truth, righteousness, peace, faith, and prayer.
  • James 4:7 says to submit to God and resist the devil. 1 Peter 5:8–9 says to be sober-minded and watchful.
  • Jesus loosed a woman from a bond (Luke 13:16), and the same Gospels also show Him healing ordinary illness without naming a demon.

A careful way to read it

  • Ask first what is physical, what is relational, what is a habit, and what is trauma. Then ask what is spiritual.
  • In Jubilees 11 the ravens were stopped with a plow. Practical defense is spiritual wisdom.
  • Do not accept a label that someone else pressures you into. Do not accept a “deliverance” that costs money, demands secrecy, or tells you to stop treatment.
  • Do not blame yourself for being targeted, and do not use “attack” to avoid a choice.

Soul Contracts, Heavenly Tablets, and Grief

This is the page I most want to get right, because it touches a real loss and a real belief.

What a soul contract is

A soul contract is the idea that before birth a soul agrees to a set of lessons, relationships, and sometimes the time and manner of its death. It comes from New Age and reincarnation-based teaching. It is not in the Bible, and it is not in 1 Enoch, Jubilees, Sirach, Wisdom, or Baruch. I will not dress it in a verse. I am saying this plainly because this book is for people at risk, and a frame that sounds biblical and is not can hurt them.

What the canon does say, which can sound close

  • God knows our days. “In your book were written, every one of them, the days that were formed for me, when as yet there was none of them” (Ps. 139:16, ESV). Job 14:5 says a person’s days are determined. This is about God’s knowledge and care, not a deal the soul made.
  • Heavenly tablets. 1 Enoch 81:1–2 and Jubilees 3:10 and 5:13 speak of things written on heavenly tablets: judgments, laws, and the names of the righteous. Jubilees 5:17–18 also says there is forgiveness written for those who turn. The tablets are not a script you signed.
  • Real choice. Sirach 15:11–20 says God left people to their own choice, set fire and water before them, and does not cause our failing. Deuteronomy 30:19 says “choose life.”
  • God does not author evil. “God cannot be tempted with evil, and he himself tempts no one” (James 1:13, ESV).
  • God can redeem harm without scripting it. “You meant evil against me, but God meant it for good” (Gen. 50:20, ESV). Joseph’s brothers chose evil. God worked good beyond it.
  • A short life can be fulfilled. Wisdom of Solomon 4:7–14 says the righteous who die early will be at rest, and 4:13 says that being perfected in a short time, he fulfilled long years. That is a hope about people God is pleased with. It is not a formula, and it does not say a death was arranged.

Why I would not build on a “fulfilled soul contract” for an addiction loss

  1. It can imply that an overdose or any death was agreed to or required, which can make God or the soul the author of something that addiction and a broken world caused.
  2. It can close the door on grief, anger, and “what if” questions that need room.
  3. For a woman still struggling who reads “her ending was written,” it can sound like “mine is too,” or “nothing I do matters.” That is the opposite of “choose life.”
  4. It makes claims about an unseen soul that no one can check.

Soul Contracts, Heavenly Tablets, and Grief (continued)

What you can honestly say instead

“[Name]’s life mattered. Their death is a real loss. I do not know everything about their last days or their soul, and I do not have to. God knew them fully (Ps. 139). The Judge of all the earth does right (Gen. 18:25). Their story moves me, and I am building something so someone else has a place to reach.”

I cannot tell you where any person stands with God, and neither can anyone else. What Scripture gives us to hold is this: He is near to the brokenhearted (Ps. 34:18, ESV), nothing “will be able to separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus our Lord” (Rom. 8:39, ESV), and He is the just and merciful judge. The canon’s hope in Wisdom 3:1–3 is that the souls of the righteous are in God’s hand. It is a hope offered, not a verdict about one person.

If you carry a belief about someone you lost

If you privately sense purpose in how a loved one’s life has shaped yours, that is your testimony and your grief, and it is yours to keep. I would label it for what it is, a personal belief and not a teaching of Scripture. If this book is ever shared with others, I would leave that belief out of the shared pages. Grief resources, if you want them: GRASP (Grief Recovery After a Substance Passing, grasphelp.org), GriefShare (griefshare.org), and 988 any time. Confirm current details.

What I am taking from the deep dive

Sunrise, sunset, day length, moonrise, and moonset depend on exactly where you stand, so they are not on the day pages yet. To add them, put one line in data/location.txt, like Austin, TX | 30.27 | -97.74 | America/Chicago, and rebuild.

Until then, use the moon’s phase, the sun’s constellation and declination, and the evening star on each page. Those are the same everywhere on Earth for a given date and hour, apart from how high the star appears in your sky.
