Welcome to the worktable

A note from Hellshaker

Hey, it's Hellshaker.

I made this kit for the version of me who didn't know where to start. I put it together with Big Boy right beside me, because he stayed beside me while I was learning how to stay beside myself.

I know what the floor feels like. Addiction. Abuse. Grief. I lost my sister, Brandi Renee, and I wish she'd had somewhere to reach. So I'm building it now, one piece at a time.

Here's what I learned: nobody rebuilds everything in a day. I tried. What works is one honest step, then the next, then the next. Then you look up and there's a whole wall of pieces you put there yourself.

This kit gives your hands something honest to do (cut, glue, write, fold, pray) while your heart catches up. It also gives you real tools: a plan for when a craving hits, a way to draw a line with people, a checklist for the paperwork of life, and seven days to walk through on purpose.

I'm not a doctor, a therapist, or a pastor. This kit is a companion, not a replacement, for real help. If you need more than paper tonight, turn to page 29 and use those numbers. Asking for help is part of the rebuild. It is not proof that you're failing.

Baby, I know what far gone looks like. Now let's see what God can do from there.

Still here. Still His. Let's rebuild.
— Hellshaker

This kit is for you if…

  • You're early in recovery, or thinking about starting.
  • You're years in and fighting a hard week.
  • You're grieving, healing from abuse, or starting over.
  • You need something to do with your hands and something true to hold.

Three moves. That's it.

  1. Make it. Cut & glue your board, cards, and bookmarks.
  2. Walk it. One page a day for 7 days (page 20).
  3. Use it. Keep the jar, the plan, and your list where you can reach them.

Each day, about 20–30 minutes

  1. Read the note. Read the Scripture twice.
  2. Write your three answers. Short is fine.
  3. Glue the day's piece(s) on your board.
  4. Read the day's word card and bookmark.
  5. Pull one slip from the jar and do it.
  6. Pray it. Check your boxes.

Rules of the week

  • Miss a day? Do the next one. Don't restart. Don't quit.
  • Rough day? Do the first box and place one piece. That counts.
  • Don't do it perfect. Do it honest.
  • Don't do it alone. Tell one safe person you're doing this.
Before you start: if you're in danger, thinking about hurting yourself, or in withdrawal from alcohol or benzodiazepines (which can be medically dangerous to stop suddenly), get medical help first. Call 988, 911, or SAMHSA at 1-800-662-4357. The rebuild will still be here. Full list on page 29.

Word cards 1–4

Cut on the dotted lines. Keep in order or shuffle. Pull one when you need a word.
#1
Identify
You can't rebuild what you keep calling something else. Name it. God already knows what it is.
“Search me, O God, and know my heart: try me, and know my thoughts.”— Psalm 139:23 (KJV)
Today's stepWrite the one thing you've been calling by a softer name. Then write what it's really called.
#2
Surrender
Surrender isn't losing. It's finally letting go of a wheel you were never built to drive.
“Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding.”— Proverbs 3:5 (KJV)
Today's stepSay it out loud: “God, I can't. You can.” Then name one thing you're handing over today.
#3
Tell the Truth
Secrets keep you sick. One true thing, told to one safe person, breaks their grip.
“Confess your faults one to another, and pray one for another, that ye may be healed.”— James 5:16 (KJV)
Today's stepPick a safe person (not someone who used you). Tell them one true thing this week. Write their name here.
#4
Release
You can't hold the chain and the hand of God at the same time. Something has to go.
“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”— Matthew 11:28 (KJV)
Today's stepWrite what you're setting down on a slip. Fold it. Drop it in the jar. Leave it there.

The Next Right Step jar

For the moments you don't know what to do next.

Set it up

  1. Cut the slips on pages 16–19. Fold each in half.
  2. Drop them in a jar. Cut a label below, tape or glue it on.
  3. Set an empty Done Jar beside it.

The rules of the jar

  1. Pull one. Don't shop for a better one. (If a slip isn't safe for you, put it back and pull again.)
  2. Do just that one, even if it's five minutes.
  3. Move it to the Done Jar. Watch it fill.

If You Slipped

Relapse is not resignation. Shame says hide. God says come.
I understand why it happened. And you are still responsible for what you do next. Both are true. Start here.

5

7HE H0LY R3UPBoundaries

Boundary Builder

Forgiveness is not access. Grace is not enabling. A boundary is not hatred.
Understanding why someone is the way they are isn't the same as letting them back in. You can love them, pray for them, and keep the gate locked.
OpenTrusted. Safe. Consistent. Full access to my life.
LimitedTime-limited, in public, or with a third person. Trust is being rebuilt.
DistanceText or brief contact only. No private time. No money. No favors.
ClosedNo contact. No explanation owed. Safety comes first.
Person / situationWhat keeps happeningAccess levelMy boundary, in one sentence & what I'll do if it's crossed

Sentences you can steal

  • “I love you, and I won't be around when you're using.”
  • “I forgive you. I'm not available for that anymore.”
  • “No, I can't. I'm not explaining.”
  • “I'll talk when we can both be calm.”
  • “I'm not lending money. That's my answer.”

Read this twice

  • You don't need permission to protect yourself.
  • Boundaries are for your behavior, not controlling theirs.
  • If someone is dangerous, don't confront them. Make a safety plan with the hotline on page 29.
  • Going back before you're ready doesn't make you weak. Start again.
