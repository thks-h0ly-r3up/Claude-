How to Read This Book§H2§

I am not a doctor, therapist, or pastor. I am a survivor who is still building, reaching back with what I have learned, what Scripture actually teaches, and something practical you can use. Four kinds of writing share these pages, and I want you to know which is which:

What Scripture says

Verses are quoted or cited by reference and read in context. Unless noted, quotations are from the ESV. When the Bible is clear, these pages say so plainly.

My testimony

Places where I am sharing what I lived and what I am still learning. It is one woman's experience — not a rule for yours, and not a finished recovery story.

Interpretation and practice

Pictures like doors, contracts, stages, and seasons are ways of seeing and tools to practice with. Christians hold different views on some of them. Test them; keep what helps.

Symbol and image

Broken chains, vineyards, anchors, and re-ups are pictures of freedom and renewal. A page of writing can help you practice something new. It does not, by itself, break a curse or change God's mind.

A few honest ground rules

  • You can skip any exercise. You can keep your eyes open. You can use a different anchor — a cold glass of water, your feet on the floor, a hand on a pet. You never have to relive something to complete a page.
  • Prayers here are conversations with God, not formulas. Saying words out loud helps many people mean them; it does not force anything.
  • Not every struggle is spiritual, and not every hard day is an attack. Sleep, food, pain, grief, and old wounds are real too. Sort them out with people who know you.
  • Forgiving someone is not the same as trusting them again. Boundaries protect; they are not punishment. A relapse does not make you disposable.
  • If a page opens more than you can carry, stop. Reach for a safe person or one of the numbers at the back. The page will wait.

1.1 The definition they never gave you§H102§

Trauma is not the event. Trauma is what the event left inside you when your nervous system could not finish the response it needed to finish.

Say it plainer: something happened that was too much, too fast, too soon, or for too long, and you had no way out and nobody to run to. So your body did the only thing it could — it froze the moment and carried it.

You are not still reacting to the past. You are reacting to the unfinished present the past left in your body.

1.2 The four responses — and why yours wasn't a choice§H103§

Response What it looks like What it protected
Fight Rage, arguing, provoking, control, "I'll ruin it before it ruins me" Your right to exist
Flight Leaving, moving, cutting off, ghosting, staying too busy to feel Your body's exit
Freeze Going blank, dissociating, watching yourself from the ceiling Your mind, when the body couldn't run
Fawn People-pleasing, over-apologizing, becoming useful to whoever is dangerous Your life, by making you not-worth-hurting

Most abuse survivors are fawners who got called "sweet." Most addiction survivors are flighters who got called "unreliable." Most children of chaos are freezers who got called "spacey."

They were not personality traits. They were survival strategies that kept working after the danger left.

1.3 The kinds of trauma — check what's yours§H104§

Mark everything that applies. Nobody is grading you. Nobody sees this but you and God.

  • Acute — one event. A wreck. An assault. A night.
  • Chronic — the same thing over and over. Years of it.
  • Complex (C-PTSD) — repeated harm inside a relationship you couldn't leave. Childhood. Marriage. Trafficking. A church.
  • Developmental — it happened while you were still being built, so it's in the foundation, not the walls.
  • Betrayal trauma — the harm came from the one who was supposed to protect you. This is the one that breaks the ability to trust itself.
  • Sexual trauma — your body was taken from you and you still live in it.
  • Religious / spiritual abuse — Scripture used as a weapon. God's name on a hand that hit you. "Submit." "Forgive." "Don't be divisive."
  • Grief trauma — a death you never got to finish grieving because life didn't stop.
  • Traumatic loss by addiction or suicide — the specific hell of losing someone to the same thing that almost took you.
  • Medical trauma — being held down, not believed, not medicated, not heard.
  • Institutional trauma — CPS, courts, jail, rehab, foster care, a system that processed you instead of helping you.
  • Moral injury — what YOU did that you can't reconcile with who you wanted to be. This is the one nobody makes workbooks for. We will.
  • Vicarious — what you absorbed carrying everybody else's.

1.4 What trauma does to the body (simplified, so you stop thinking you're broken)§H105§

Your body is not malfunctioning. It is running an old program on a current threat setting.

  • Amygdala — your smoke alarm. After trauma it's set to go off at toast. Hypervigilance, startle, "I can read a room in three seconds."
  • Hippocampus — your filing clerk. Trauma disrupts it, so the memory never gets filed as past. That's why it feels like now. That's a flashback. It is not weakness and it is not demonic — it is a filing error with a body attached.
  • Prefrontal cortex — your reasoning. It goes offline under threat. That's why you "know better" and still do it. Knowing lives upstairs; the alarm lives downstairs; downstairs wins.
  • Vagus nerve — your brake pedal. Trauma wears the brake. That's the shutdown, the numbness, the sudden exhaustion.
  • Cortisol and adrenaline — the fuel. Years of it is why your stomach, your immune system, your sleep, your cycle, your teeth, your back all went down at once.

Trauma leaves its mark on the body. Talking helps, and for many people it is not enough by itself. You cannot think your way out of a body-level wound. You have to bring the body.

Body-based resets (use these all through the workbook)

Learn these NOW, before the hard pages.

1. The 4-7-8 breath (drops the alarm) In through the nose 4 counts. Hold 7. Out through the mouth 8, long and loud. Four rounds. The long exhale is the part that works.

2. 5-4-3-2-1 (ends a flashback) Out loud: 5 things you see, 4 you can touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, 1 you taste. Then say the date and your age. "It is [today's date]. I am [your age]. That was then."

3. Cold water on the wrists and face (hard reset) Can slow the heart rate fast. Works for many people when other things do not. (If you have a heart condition, ask your doctor before using cold water on your face.)

4. Orienting (tells the body the room is safe) Slowly turn your head and look at every corner of the room. Let your eyes land on the exit. Your body needs to SEE there's a door.

5. Push the wall Palms flat on a wall, push hard for 10 seconds, release. Gives the fight response somewhere to go.

6. Name it to tame it "I am having a trauma response. This is not danger. This is memory."

1.6 The part nobody will say out loud§H107§

Some of what happened to you was not your fault AND some of what you did afterward was. Both are true at once. Holding only the first makes you a permanent victim. Holding only the second makes you a person who can't stop punishing themselves.

You need both hands.

WORK PAGE 5 — The two columns

What was DONE to me (not mine to carry) What I DID (mine to own and be forgiven for)

Look at column one. Say: "I set this down. It was never mine."

Look at column two. Say: "I own this. I am not condemned for it. I will make it right where I can."

That's it. That's the whole difference between shame and repentance. Shame says I am bad. Repentance says I did bad, and I'm not staying there.

The Spiritual Realm: The Crash Course§H108§

2.1 Start here: there are two realms and one is more real§H109§

The physical realm is what you can measure. The spiritual realm is what generates it.

"We look not to the things that are seen but to the things that are unseen. For the things that are seen are transient, but the things that are unseen are eternal." — 2 Corinthians 4:18

"For we do not wrestle against flesh and blood, but against the rulers, against the authorities, against the cosmic powers over this present darkness." — Ephesians 6:12

Paul is describing something real: an opposing spiritual reality, with structure and strategy — and a limit. Christians hold a range of views on the details, and Scripture gives us less of a map than we sometimes wish. What it gives us clearly is Jesus.

2.2 The three-way error most people make§H110§

Error What it sounds like Why it wrecks you
Everything is demonic "The devil gave me depression." You spend years binding a spirit that was a thyroid, a trauma loop, or a bad marriage. Nothing improves, so you conclude God failed.
Nothing is spiritual "It's all just psychology." You white-knuckle a war you can't see and lose ground you don't know you're standing on.
It's spiritual so I don't need help "I just need more faith." You die of something treatable while praying.

Our working position: it is often more than one thing at once, and you work on every part that is there.

A wound can be real trauma, and it can also be a place where lies and temptation find footing. A craving can be real neurochemistry, and it can also be a pull toward a false comfort. Treat the body. Tend the wound. Take the spiritual side to God. You do not have to decide which one it "really" is before you start getting help.

2.3 What's actually in the realm§H111§

God — not a force, a Person. Sovereign, present, the only one with unlimited authority. Not the author of what was done to you.

The Holy Spirit — God living in you if you're His. Your comfort, your conviction, your power, your discernment. Conviction feels like clean grief and points to a specific thing with a way out. Condemnation feels like drowning and points at your whole self with no exit. Learn the difference. It will save you years.

Jesus — the one who already won. Colossians 2:15: He disarmed the powers and made a public spectacle of them. This matters because it means you are not fighting for a victory — you are enforcing one.

Angels — messengers and warriors. Sent. Not summoned by you, not worshiped by you.

Satan — a created being. Fallen. Limited. A liar (John 8:44), a thief (John 10:10), an accuser (Revelation 12:10). He is not omnipresent and he is not the opposite of God. Scripture never says he can read minds; only God knows the heart (1 Kings 8:39).

The enemy's forces — Ephesians 6:12 names rulers, authorities, cosmic powers, and spiritual forces, and Colossians 2:15 says Christ disarmed them. Scripture says far less about how they work than people online pretend. You do not need a map of ranks. You need Jesus, honest people, and discernment — and you need to hold back from calling a hard experience demonic just because it is hard. A pattern that follows you from place to place, or runs through a whole family, is worth taking seriously, and worth bringing to a doctor, a counselor, and a trusted pastor together.

Your own flesh — Galatians 5. Not a demon. Your untrained appetite. Most of what people try to cast out, they need to crucify. You cannot rebuke a habit you keep feeding.

WORK PAGE 6 — Sorting your own situation

2.4 What keeps feeding the struggle§H112§

Some struggles keep getting fed by the same things. Naming them is not meant to shame you — it gives you something you can change. Christians disagree about how much of this is spiritual, so use what helps and hold the rest loosely.

Check honestly:

  • Unforgiveness / bitterness (Matthew 18:34–35 — the jailer. Hebrews 12:15 — the root.) Very common in survivors, and very understandable.
  • Agreement with a lie — "I'm worthless" is a lie you have been rehearsing, and it needs replacing with truth.
  • Occult contact — tarot, mediums, Ouija, spells, santería, crystals for power, "harmless" psychics, astrology as authority, rituals you did as a kid for protection because nobody else was protecting you.
  • Sexual entanglements ("soul ties") — 1 Corinthians 6:16 says sexual union joins two people. What was done to you against your will was a wrong done to you, not a sin of yours — and it can still leave a hold on your mind and body that is worth bringing to God and a counselor. ("Soul tie" is not a phrase in Scripture; it is a name many believers use for this experience.)
  • Family patterns — sin and wounds running through generations (Exodus 20:5, Lamentations 5:7).
  • Vows and self-curses — "I will never love anyone again." "I'll always be like my mother." "I hope I die." Those are promises you made in pain.
  • Substances — altering the mind lowers your guard, and addiction can rule a life like an idol. (Galatians 5:20 lists sorcery — pharmakeia — next to idolatry; Scripture's concern is what rules you, not medicine taken under a doctor's care.)
  • Trauma itself — a wound can leave you more exposed and more alone. That is not your fault, and it is something you are allowed to tend.
  • What you consume — what you watch, hear, scroll, and replay at night is a diet, and it feeds something.
  • Things done or spoken over you by others — dedications, harsh words from family, things you don't fully remember.

2.5 Your actual authority — read this twice§H113§

You are not begging. You are not negotiating. You are not equal-and-opposite.

  • Luke 10:19 — "I have given you authority to tread on serpents and scorpions... and nothing shall hurt you."
  • Ephesians 2:6 — you are seated with Him in the heavenly places. Positional. Above, not below.
  • James 4:7 — submit to God, THEN resist the devil. That order is not optional. Resisting without submitting is how people get hurt.
  • 1 John 4:4 — greater is He in you than he in the world.
  • Revelation 12:11 — they conquered by the blood of the Lamb and the word of their testimony. Your story matters. Telling the truth about what happened to you — to safe people, at your own pace — takes the power out of secrecy.

What authority is not: a volume contest. You don't need to scream. Jesus rebuked with a sentence. Loud is for your own fear, not for their ears.

2.6 Praying it through — renouncing, forgiving, and handing it to God§H114§

Before you pray this: these prayers are a faith practice, not a formula. Scripture invites us to confess, forgive, resist the devil, and pray out loud with others (James 4:7; 5:16), but it does not hand us a script, and nothing here replaces a doctor, a counselor, or a pastor who knows you. You are allowed to skip any step that stirs up more than you can hold. Saying the words aloud helps many people mean them. Do it with one trustworthy believer present if you can. If you can't, you can still pray it alone.

Step 1 — Get under cover.

"Father, I come to You through Jesus Christ, who died for me and rose again. I am Yours. I submit myself to You — my body, my mind, my memory, my will, my future. I need You for this, not my own strength. Cover me in the blood of Jesus."

Step 2 — Confess what's yours.

"I confess my part. Not what was done to me — my part. [Name it specifically. No lists of 'anything and everything.' Be specific. Specific is where power is.] I repent. I turn from it. I receive Your forgiveness, and I refuse to keep paying for what You already paid for."

Step 3 — Forgive, by name.

"I choose to forgive [name]. Not because it was okay. It was not okay. Because I refuse to be their jailer and their prisoner at the same time. I release them to You and out of my hands. I take back what I gave them access to. I cancel the debt I have been trying to collect."

(This will not feel true the first time. Do it anyway. Feeling follows obedience, sometimes by months.)

Step 4 — Renounce what you've named.

"In the name of Jesus Christ I renounce [each item from Work Page 7, one at a time, out loud]. I break every agreement I made with it, knowingly or unknowingly. I take my hand off it, and I ask You to close that door and seal it with the blood of Jesus."

3.3 The cost of being chosen — the unfiltered list§H119§

3.5 The transition: from victim to survivor to steward§H121§

Part IV

Survivor of Addiction and Abuse§H122§

7HE H0LY R3UP

4.1 The truth about how the two braid together§H123§

Addiction and abuse are not two separate problems that happened to the same person. They feed each other.

Abuse creates the hole. Addiction fills it. Addiction then puts you in rooms where more abuse happens. More abuse deepens the hole. Around and around.

This is why "just stop using" fails. You are not asking someone to drop a habit. You are asking them to sit in an unmedicated wound with no skills and no safety. Of course they went back. Anyone would.

Which means: you cannot get sober and heal the trauma later. You have to run them together. Sobriety without trauma work is a countdown. Trauma work while actively using is a spin cycle. Both, at once, slowly.

4.2 What addiction actually is§H124§

Three layers. Work all three or it comes back.

Layer 1 — Physical. Real dependence. Real receptors. Real withdrawal. Requires medical help sometimes. Not a moral issue.

Layer 2 — Psychological. The relief. Addiction is not a love of the substance. It is a love of the gap in the pain. It worked. That's the problem — it worked, right up until it started charging interest.

Layer 3 — Spiritual. Every addiction is a false savior. It promises what only God gives: peace, escape, belonging, numbness from shame, courage, rest. It's a counterfeit communion.

"My people have committed two evils: they have forsaken me, the fountain of living waters, and dug for themselves cisterns — broken cisterns that can hold no water." — Jeremiah 2:13

The cistern isn't the sin. Digging your own is.

WORK PAGE 13 — What was it doing for you?

4.3 Abuse: naming it without softening it§H125§

  • Controlled — money, phone, car, friends, food, sleep

  • Degraded, mocked, called names until you believed them

The specific damage of betrayal by family

If the hand that hurt you was supposed to feed you, you didn't just get injured. Your instrument for detecting safety got miscalibrated at the factory. That's why you can smell danger from a mile away and still walk straight into it. Your alarm works. Your identifier is broken. You learned that harm and love arrive in the same package.

That is fixable. It is not fixable by trying harder. It is fixable by building new evidence, slowly, with safe people, over time.

4.4 Moral injury — the page nobody writes§H126§

Some of you are not haunted by what was done to you. You're haunted by what you did while you were surviving.

7.1 Why letting go is the hardest page in the book§H148§

Because you're not letting go of a person. You're letting go of:

  • the version of them you needed them to become
  • the apology you're still owed
  • the hope that this time it would be different
  • the role you've played your whole life
  • and the belief that if you hold on hard enough, love will finally show up

You are grieving something that never existed. That's why it hurts worse than a funeral. There's no body and nobody sends flowers.

7.5 How to actually do it — the release§H152§

Don't do this halfway. Halfway is how you end up doing it fourteen times.

Step 3 — Forgive without reconciling. Forgiveness is between you and God. Reconciliation requires two people and repentance. You can be fully forgiving and fully unavailable. Nobody taught us that and it changes everything.

7.6 Sitting Silent with God§H153§

This is the last section because it's the one that holds everything else up. And it's the one you'll resist most, because silence is where the pain lives and you've spent your whole life outrunning it.

Why silence is the hardest spiritual discipline for survivors

Noise was safety. If it was quiet, you could hear them coming. If it was quiet, you had to feel it. So you filled every second — music, TV, scrolling, people, work, using. You are not undisciplined. You are guarded.

Silence will feel like danger before it feels like peace. That's not failure. That's a nervous system that learned quiet meant something bad was about to happen.

What the Scripture actually says

  • 1 Kings 19:11–12 — Elijah, suicidal and burned out, looks for God in wind, earthquake, fire. God's in "a sound of thin silence." The whisper comes after God feeds him and lets him sleep twice. Note the order: food, sleep, then the whisper. God did not rebuke his exhaustion. He fed it.
  • Psalm 46:10 — "Be still and know." The Hebrew rapha is closer to "let go, drop your hands, stop striving."
  • Psalm 62:1 — "For God alone my soul waits in silence."
  • Lamentations 3:26 — "It is good that one should wait quietly for the salvation of the LORD."
  • Isaiah 30:15 — "In returning and rest you shall be saved; in quietness and in trust shall be your strength." And then, brutally honest: "But you were unwilling."

THE PRACTICE — start absurdly small

Week 1 — 2 minutes a day. Yes, two. Sit. Feet on the floor. Eyes open if closed feels unsafe — that's allowed and it's smart. Say once: "Jesus, I'm here. You don't have to say anything. I'm not going anywhere for two minutes."

Then nothing. No agenda. No asking. No performance.

When your mind runs — and it will — don't fight it. Say "later" and come back. You'll come back forty times in two minutes. Coming back IS the practice. The returning is the prayer.

Week 2 — 5 minutes. Week 3 — 10 minutes. Week 4 — 15 minutes. Stay here. 15 minutes daily beats an hour on Sunday.

The four postures of silence

Rotate these. Don't do just one.

  1. Silent presence — nothing. Just there. "I'm here."
  2. Silent listening — one question, then quiet. "What do You want me to know today?" Then wait. Write down what comes; test it later against Scripture and wise people. If it accuses, shames, or condemns you, it isn't Him.
  3. Silent surrender — hands open, palms up. Physically hand over one thing. Name it. Leave it. Go.
  4. Silent lament — sit with the grief without fixing it. Don't explain it to God. Don't excuse Him. Just bring it. Psalm 88 ends in darkness and God still printed it.
