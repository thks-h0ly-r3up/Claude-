### 2. SCRIPTURE AND TEACHING

> "The LORD is near to the brokenhearted and saves the crushed in spirit."
> Psalm 34:18 (ESV)

**What Scripture says.** God is *near* to people who are brokenhearted. Notice what the verse does not say. It does not say the broken person fixed themselves first, or prayed the right way, or had enough faith.

**The context.** The heading above Psalm 34 ties it to a low moment in David's life, when he was running for his life and pretending to be out of his mind in front of a foreign king (1 Samuel 21:10-15). The psalm was written by a man who knew what it was to be cornered. It is not the work of someone far from the floor.

**The word.** "Brokenhearted" comes from the Hebrew root *shabar*, which means to break or shatter. Same family of word, different pieces. It is an honest word for what grief, loss, and harm do to a person.

**Interpretation and application (this part is me, not the verse).** I know why you learned to survive the way you did. I also know there is a day you stop asking "how did I end up here" and start asking "what happens next." Both questions are allowed. God being near does not mean everything is fixed today. It means you are not alone in what is still broken, and you can tell Him the truth before you know how to clean it up.

Nearness is not a replacement for help. If you are in danger, in withdrawal, or thinking about ending your life, call or text 988 (U.S.), call 911, or reach someone safe right now. Seeking care is not a failure of faith.

**What happens in the body.** When you are overwhelmed, your nervous system can go into survival mode, with a racing heart, numbness, shaking, or a heavy shutdown. That is not weak faith. It is a body trying to protect you. It does not explain everyone the same way, and you do not have to diagnose yourself to take one small step.

**Declarations (grounded in the verse):**
- *I can come to God as I am.*
- *The LORD is near to the brokenhearted.*
- *This is where I start. It is not where I end up, and I do not have to see the whole road today.*

### 4. SOFT ACTION (pick one, not all)

- Drink some water or eat something small.
- Text or call one safe person: "I am having a hard day. Can you check on me later?"
- Write down one next real thing, like treatment, a meeting, a shelter, a doctor, or a hotline, and set a time to do it.
- Color today's page.

If you have no safe person right now, that is a real thing, and it is not your fault. Use a hotline or a local help line as your first support.

### 5. SUPPLICATION
