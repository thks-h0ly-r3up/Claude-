What’s Ahead

Seven days. Each day takes about 15 to 30 minutes, and you can do it in pieces. You can skip any exercise, do it your own way, or come back to it later. Nothing here asks you to relive your story.

Each day has five parts

  1. Sanctuary: a short, optional grounding exercise and a prayer for presence.
  2. Scripture & Teaching: a passage in context, plain explanation, and application.
  3. Sacred Reflection: three prompts with space to write. Grief, anger, doubt, and hope are all welcome.
  4. Soft Action: one small, realistic step. Not a life overhaul.
  5. Supplication: a guided prayer for when you can't find your own words.

Introduction: Built on the Floor

This was not built on a clean stage. It was built on the floor.

I lost my sister to addiction. I have survived homelessness, trafficking, abuse, addiction, and more than a decade of manipulation in a destructive relationship. There came a point when I was alone, terrified of people, and struggling to trust anybody. When I reached for help, too often I found strings attached: people who could talk recovery and quote Scripture and still see somebody's wounds as a business opportunity, and places that handed me a watered-down verse and told me to pray it away.

I needed somewhere that could hold the truth about trauma without pushing God out of the room. Somewhere I could tell the truth without being treated like a project, a paycheck, or a failure of faith. I wish my sister had somewhere like that to reach. I can't say it would have changed her outcome. I can build toward making that kind of support easier to find for somebody who is still here.

My American Bully, Big Boy, stood beside me through some of my loneliest days. God met me while I was still trying to get up. I know what it means to go looking for another plug. In my own testimony I call God the OG Plug, my way of pointing old language toward the Source of my hope. That is my metaphor, not a title from Scripture.

I am building as I go. I have not arrived, and I am not qualified to treat anybody. I'm a woman reaching back with what I've learned, what Scripture actually teaches, and something practical you can use.

You survived. Now we rebuild. Brokenness is not the destination. Baby, you do not have to rebuild your whole life before sundown. But we do need to choose what happens next.

— Nikki “Hellshaker” Heller

Before You Begin

Safety first

If you are thinking about hurting yourself, may have overdosed, or are in danger right now, stop and call 911 or call/text 988. Everything else can wait.

Your permissions

  • You may keep your eyes open, skip an exercise, or do a different one.
  • You may write one line instead of five. You may write nothing and just pray.
  • You never have to write down details that hurt. Use words like “that night” or “what happened.”
  • If a page stirs up more than you can hold, stop and reach for support: a counselor, a trusted person, 988, or SAMHSA (1-800-662-4357).
  • If you can only do one part a day, do the Soft Action.

If your situation is hard right now

No phone, no privacy, no money, unstable housing, no ride, or nobody to call are real limits. Each day offers options that fit fewer resources. Dialing 211 connects many people with local food, shelter, and clinic help. A library can offer a phone, computer, and quiet.

Faith and practical help

Prayer belongs beside treatment, medical care, safe housing, and safety planning. It does not replace them. If you have been using heavily every day, do not stop suddenly on your own; withdrawal from some substances can be dangerous. Call a doctor, an ER, or SAMHSA first.

Tell God the Truth Before You Know How to Clean It Up

1 • Sanctuary (optional)

Look around and name five things you can see. Keep your eyes open the whole time. If that doesn't help, hold something cool or textured instead, or skip this and go straight to the Scripture.

Prayer for presence: Lord, whatever is true about where I am right now, be in it with me. Amen.

2 • Scripture & Teaching

The heading of Psalm 142 places David in a cave. It is a prayer with no polish on it.

Psalm 142:1-2 (ESV)With my voice I cry out to the LORD; with my voice I plead for mercy to the LORD. I pour out my complaint before him; I tell my trouble before him.
Psalm 142:4-5 (ESV)Look to the right and see: there is none who takes notice of me; no refuge remains to me; no one cares for my soul. I cry to you, O LORD; I say, “You are my refuge, my portion in the land of the living.”

Scripture says David doesn't clean this up before he prays it. He names his trouble, his fear, and his loneliness (“no one cares for my soul”) and then turns toward God as his refuge in the same breath. He also says that in the path he walks, others have “hidden a trap” for him (verse 3). The Bible does not hide how raw honest prayer can be.

My story For a long time I thought I had to get myself together before I could pray. I didn't know how to be honest with God and honest with people at the same time. I still don't always.

Application You are allowed to start with the mess. Honesty with God is where you begin, not where you stay: the next step is usually a person, a plan, and a place to get help. David's feeling that no one cares is real to him, and it is not God's verdict on your worth.

“I can tell God the truth before I know how to clean it up.”
“Honesty is where I start. It is not where I have to stay.”
“I do not have to explain my pain to be allowed to bring it.”
#human Shame makes the body brace and the mouth go quiet.  #truthmode God invites honest complaint. It is not faithlessness.  #futureyou Truth-telling is the first brick of anything I rebuild.

Day 2 of 7 • Read

The God Who Sees

1 • Sanctuary (optional)

Place a hand on something nearby: a table, your leg, a blanket. Notice its temperature and texture. Keep your eyes open if you like. Skip this if touch isn't comfortable and try listening for three sounds instead.

Prayer for presence: Lord, You see where I am. Let me know I am not invisible. Amen.

2 • Scripture & Teaching

Hagar was an Egyptian servant in Abram and Sarai's household. She was treated harshly, and she ran.

Genesis 16:7-8 (ESV)The angel of the LORD found her by a spring of water in the wilderness… And he said, “Hagar, servant of Sarai, where have you come from and where are you going?” She said, “I am fleeing from my mistress Sarai.”
Genesis 16:13 (ESV)So she called the name of the LORD who spoke to her, “You are a God of seeing,” for she said, “Truly here I have seen him who looks after me.”

Scripture says Sarai gave Hagar to Abram to bear a child, and when Hagar conceived, Sarai “dealt harshly with her, and she fled” (verse 6). God finds Hagar alone in the wilderness, calls her by name, and asks her what is true: where she has come from and where she is going. She answers honestly. Then she names God “a God of seeing.” The text also records that the angel told her to return (verse 9). That is what happened to her in her story. It is not an instruction for anyone in danger today to go back to someone who hurts them.

Application When you have been used, controlled, or ignored, it is easy to believe nobody saw it. Hagar's story says God saw someone with no power, no status, and no safe place to go. Being seen does not mean everything is fixed. It means you are not alone in it, and it can be the start of getting safe.

Application Safety is not disobedience. Leaving a dangerous situation is not simple, and no one gets to shame you for how long it takes. If someone is hurting or controlling you, a trained advocate can help you plan: National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233 (text START to 88788) or the National Human Trafficking Hotline 1-888-373-7888 (text 233733).

“God sees me where I am.”
“Getting safe is wise. It is not weak.”
“Forgiving someone does not mean I owe them access to me.”
#human A body that has been controlled learns to stay small and watch the room.  #truthmode Scripture records what happened to Hagar. It does not tell survivors to stay in danger.  #futureyou Safety first. Then stability. Then strength.

Bread, Water, and Rest

1 • Sanctuary (optional)

Breathe in through your nose for a count of four and out slowly for six, three times, eyes open. If breathing exercises make you feel worse, skip them: press your feet into the floor or splash cool water on your hands instead.

Prayer for presence: Lord, meet my body where it is tonight. Amen.

2 • Scripture & Teaching

Elijah had just seen God do enormous things. Then Queen Jezebel threatened his life, and he ran.

1 Kings 19:4-5 (ESV)But he himself went a day's journey into the wilderness and came and sat down under a broom tree. And he asked that he might die… And he lay down and slept under a broom tree. And behold, an angel touched him and said to him, “Arise and eat.”
1 Kings 19:7 (ESV)And the angel of the LORD came again a second time and touched him and said, “Arise and eat, for the journey is too great for you.”

Scripture says Elijah is afraid, exhausted, and asking to die. God's first response is not a lecture. It is food, water, and sleep, twice, and then the journey continues (verses 5-8). Later, God meets him not in the wind, the earthquake, or the fire, but in “a low whisper” (verses 11-13).

Application Here is a plain-language way to think about it, not a diagnosis: when a body decides it is in danger, it can push you toward fighting, running, freezing, or shutting down. After long stretches of that, people often crash into exhaustion, numbness, or despair. That is not weak faith. It is a body that has been carrying too much.

Application So tend the body. Water, real food, sleep, medication as prescribed, and medical care matter. If you have been drinking heavily or using opioids, benzodiazepines, or other drugs every day, do not stop suddenly on your own: withdrawal from some substances can be dangerous. Call a doctor, an ER, or SAMHSA (1-800-662-4357) first. Prayer belongs beside that care, not in place of it.

“Rest is not laziness.”
“My body has been carrying a lot.”
“Needing help with the basics does not make me a failure.”
#human Exhaustion can look like apathy, anger, or hopelessness.  #truthmode God tends Elijah's body before addressing his despair.  #futureyou Stability starts with sleep, food, and someone who knows my plan.

When I Don't Do What I Want

1 • Sanctuary (optional)

Press your feet flat on the floor and notice the weight of your body in the chair or bed. Look at a color in the room and name it. Keep your eyes open. If your body wants to move instead, stand and stretch.

Prayer for presence: Lord, I'm here, and so are You. Let me be honest without being crushed. Amen.

2 • Scripture & Teaching

Paul wrote to believers in Rome. In this passage he describes an honest, painful struggle with doing what he does not want to do.

Romans 7:15, 19 (ESV)For I do not understand my own actions. For I do not do what I want, but I do the very thing I hate… For I do not do the good I want, but the evil I do not want is what I keep on doing.
Romans 7:24-25a (ESV)Wretched man that I am! Who will deliver me from this body of death? Thanks be to God through Jesus Christ our Lord!
Romans 8:1 (ESV)There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus.

Scripture says Paul does not hide the struggle. He also does not stay in it: the answer to “who will deliver me?” is “thanks be to God through Jesus Christ,” and chapter 8 begins with “no condemnation.” Christians disagree about exactly which stage of Paul's life this passage describes, so hold that with humility. What is plain is that honest struggle and Christ as deliverer sit side by side.

Application A craving is a signal, not a verdict. It usually rises, peaks, and passes. What you do next is where your choices live. “No condemnation” does not mean “no consequences,” and understanding why you struggle is not the same as excusing what you do. Both are true: I understand why you learned to survive like that, and we have to look at what it is costing you now.

Application Relapse does not make anyone disposable. It is information: where the plan had a hole, what the trigger was, who you didn't call. Willpower alone runs out. Support, a plan, and honest people do not.

“A craving is a signal, not a verdict.”
“There is no condemnation for me in Christ, and there is still work to do.”
“I do not have to fight this alone.”
#human Cravings and shame can hit the body at the same time.  #truthmode Honest struggle is in the Bible. Excuses are not.  #futureyou Small, repeated choices with support become a pattern.

Jesus Wept

1 • Sanctuary (optional)

Put one hand on your chest or your stomach and notice it move as you breathe, without changing it. Keep your eyes open. If this brings up too much, put your hand on a table instead or skip it.

Prayer for presence: Lord, be with me in the grief. I don't need to explain it. Amen.

2 • Scripture & Teaching

Lazarus, the brother of Mary and Martha, has died. When Jesus arrives, Lazarus has already been in the tomb four days.

John 11:32-33 (ESV)Now when Mary came to where Jesus was and saw him, she fell at his feet, saying to him, “Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died.” When Jesus saw her weeping, and the Jews who had come with her also weeping, he was deeply moved in his spirit and greatly troubled.
John 11:34-35 (ESV)And he said, “Where have you laid him?” They said to him, “Lord, come and see.” Jesus wept.

Scripture says Mary says out loud what many people feel: “If you had been here.” Jesus does not correct her. He is deeply moved and troubled, and He weeps, even though the story goes on to show He will raise Lazarus. Tears and belief are in the same scene. The passage does not say every loved one is restored in this life, and it does not explain why some people are not.

My story I lost my sister to addiction. I can't tell you why God allowed it, and I won't pretend to. What I know is that He can meet you in the grief without a tidy explanation. I wish she had somewhere to reach, and I can't say it would have changed her outcome.

Application You do not have to hurry grief, clean it up, or turn it into a lesson. Anger and questions can be part of it. You may also be grieving years, safety, versions of yourself, or people who are still alive but not safe. If grief turns toward thoughts of hurting yourself, please call or text 988.

“Jesus makes room for grief.”
“I do not have to explain my pain to be allowed to feel it.”
“Missing them does not mean I'm stuck.”
#human Grief lives in the body: heavy chest, tired eyes, sudden waves.  #truthmode “Everything happens for a reason” is not in this passage. Jesus weeps.  #futureyou Honoring what I lost can be part of what I build.

Wise as Serpents: Trust Is Not Automatic

1 • Sanctuary (optional)

Notice where your body feels most tense and name it. Then find something in the room that feels steady and look at it for ten seconds. Keep your eyes open. Skip if it isn't helping.

Prayer for presence: Lord, give me wisdom, and keep me from shame while I learn. Amen.

2 • Scripture & Teaching

Jesus was loved and followed by crowds. John tells us something surprising about how He handled trust.

John 2:24-25 (ESV)But Jesus on his part did not entrust himself to them, because he knew all people and needed no one to bear witness about man, for he himself knew what was in man.
Matthew 10:16 (ESV)Behold, I am sending you out as sheep in the midst of wolves, so be wise as serpents and innocent as doves.

Scripture says In John 2:23-25, many people believed in Jesus because of the signs, but He “did not entrust himself to them.” He loved them and He was still discerning. In Matthew 10:16 He tells His disciples to be wise as serpents and innocent as doves. Paul also writes: “If possible, so far as it depends on you, live peaceably with all” (Romans 12:18), and Colossians 3:13 calls believers to forgive as the Lord forgave.

Application Forgiveness releases a debt. Trust is rebuilt by consistent, safe behavior over time. You can forgive someone and never give them access to you again. Boundaries protect; they are not punishment. Nobody gets to use Scripture to pressure you to stay in danger or hand your safety back to someone who has not changed.

Application If you have been hurt by people who used faith to control you, that is spiritual abuse, and it is not what Jesus does. And where you did harm, understanding your history is not an excuse: safe accountability, repair when it is safe, and changed behavior matter. Leaving danger is not simple. A hotline advocate can help you plan.

“Forgiveness does not automatically restore trust or access.”
“A boundary can protect without becoming punishment.”
“I can be kind and careful at the same time.”
#human Trauma can make trust feel like a trap or a test I keep failing.  #truthmode Jesus loved people and did not entrust Himself to everyone.  #futureyou Discernment is a skill I can practice.

Tended Ground

1 • Sanctuary (optional)

Notice something growing or alive nearby: a plant, a tree through a window, even your own pulse. Look at it and take one slow breath. Keep your eyes open. If nothing is near, look at your hands.

Prayer for presence: Lord, meet me here, and help me stay connected to You. Amen.

2 • Scripture & Teaching

On the night before His crucifixion, Jesus told His disciples He was the vine, and they were the branches.

John 15:1-2 (ESV)I am the true vine, and my Father is the vinedresser. Every branch in me that does not bear fruit he takes away, and every branch that does bear fruit he prunes, that it may bear more fruit.
John 15:4 (ESV)Abide in me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit by itself, unless it abides in the vine, neither can you, unless you abide in me.

Scripture says The center of the passage is abiding: staying connected to Jesus. Fruit comes from that connection, not from striving alone. The vinedresser prunes the branches that bear fruit “that it may bear more fruit” (verse 2). A few lines later, Jesus says, “apart from me you can do nothing” (verse 5).

Application A vineyard is not a bando. It is tended over seasons: watered, trimmed, protected, waited on. Pruning in a vine is cutting away what drains it. I don't read that as God causing what was done to you; Scripture doesn't say that. It is about what needs to be released so growth can happen. Isaiah 61:4 speaks of rebuilding “the ancient ruins,” a promise given to Israel that I take as an encouragement and not a personal guarantee.

Application You will not rebuild everything by sundown. Choose a daily anchor, a weekly source of support, and one thing you're building. Then repeat. You survived. Now we rebuild, one tended day at a time.

“I stay connected one day at a time.”
“Growth takes seasons, and I can be patient with mine.”
“I don't have to rebuild everything by sundown.”
#human Progress can feel invisible, and that is normal.  #truthmode Abiding is the point. Fruit is what follows, not what I force.  #futureyou A small anchor kept daily builds peace, stability, and purpose.

Making the first call

  1. Say: “I'm looking for help with (drugs / alcohol / safety / housing). Where do I start?”
  2. Ask about cost and insurance. Many places have sliding scales or free options.
  3. Ask about medical detox if you've been using daily.
  4. Write down names and numbers as you go. Bring a person if you can.
  5. If the first call doesn't work, call another. You only need one yes.
