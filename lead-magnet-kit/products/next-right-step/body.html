Day 1: Tell the Truth

Psalm 34:18 (ESV)“The LORD is near to the brokenhearted and saves the crushed in spirit.”
1 John 1:9 (ESV)“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

Nobody gets free by getting better at hiding. Most of us got real good at “I'm fine.” Fine kept the questions away. Fine kept the peace. And fine kept us right where we were.

So day one is not about fixing anything. It's about naming it. Out loud. On paper. To God, who already knows, and to one safe person, who doesn't yet. That's it. That's the whole assignment.

Telling the truth is not the same as beating yourself up. Shame says you are the problem. Truth says this is the problem, and I'm not going to carry it alone anymore. Shame keeps you hiding. Truth lets God in.

And here's the balance we hold in this house: I understand why you became this way. And you are still responsible for what you do next. Both are true. Understanding is not excusing. Explaining is not permission.

Not this (the lie)But this (the truth)
“If they knew everything, they'd leave.”Some people might. The right ones won't. Start with one safe person, not everybody.
“I have to fix it before I tell anybody.”You tell the truth so you don't have to fix it alone.
“God's mad at me.”He is near to the brokenhearted. Psalm 34:18 does not say ‘near to the ones who cleaned up first.’

Day 2: Get Safe

Psalm 46:1 (ESV)“God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble.”
Psalm 40:2 (ESV)“He drew me up from the pit of destruction, out of the miry bog, and set my feet upon a rock, making my steps secure.”

Before strategy, before feelings, before anything else: are you safe tonight? From a substance. From a person. From yourself. Safety comes first, and it's not weakness to say so.

Get practical. Put three people and three numbers where you can reach them without thinking. When you're in the middle of a bad hour, you will not be creative. You'll grab whatever is closest. So make the right thing the closest thing.

If someone is hurting you, controlling you, or forcing you, that is not your fault and it is not a failure of faith. Leaving is not simple. Nobody gets to shame you for how long it takes. When you're ready, and only when it's safe, there are people trained to help you plan it.

And hear me on this: forgiveness is not access. You can forgive somebody and never let them back in your life. You never owe anyone a seat at your table because they said sorry.

Not this (the lie)But this (the truth)
“A good Christian doesn't need a safety plan.”Faith and preparation work together. Noah still built the ark.
“I should be able to handle this alone.”Refuge means somewhere to run. Use it.
“If I leave, I'm giving up on them.”Protecting yourself is not hatred. It's stewardship of what God gave you.

Day 3: Feed the Body

1 Kings 19:6 (ESV)“And he looked, and behold, there was at his head a cake baked on hot stones and a jar of water. And he ate and drank and lay down again.”
Matthew 11:28 (ESV)“Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Elijah was worn out, afraid, and begging God to let him die. And the first thing God did wasn't a sermon. It was bread, water, and a nap. Sometimes the holy thing is a sandwich and eight hours of sleep.

Your body has been carrying a lot. If you've been using, not eating, not sleeping, or living on adrenaline, your body is running on empty, and an empty body makes everything feel worse: the cravings, the fear, the anger, the lies.

Important safety truth: if you've been drinking heavily or using benzodiazepines, opioids, or other substances daily, do not just stop cold on your own. Withdrawal from some of these can be dangerous, even deadly. Call a doctor, an ER, or the SAMHSA helpline (1-800-662-4357) first. That's not weak. That's wise.

This is where faith and practical help hold hands. Pray and take your medication as prescribed. Pray and go to the appointment. God gave doctors, nurses, and counselors. Use them.

Not this (the lie)But this (the truth)
“Real faith means I don't need a doctor.”Luke was a physician and he's in your Bible.
“I'll quit alone. I don't need anybody.”Not all withdrawals are safe alone. Get medical guidance first.
“Rest is laziness.”Jesus slept in a boat in a storm. Rest is obedience.

When you're Hungry, Angry, Lonely, or Tired, everything is harder. Check these before you make any big decision.

Set a bedtime and put the phone across the room
Call or book one medical or treatment contact

Day 4: Know Your Triggers

1 Corinthians 10:13 (ESV)“No temptation has overtaken you that is not common to man. God is faithful, and he will not let you be tempted beyond your ability, but with the temptation he will also provide the way of escape, that you may be able to endure it.”
Lamentations 3:22-23 (ESV)“The steadfast love of the LORD never ceases; his mercies never come to an end; they are new every morning; great is your faithfulness.”

The corner didn't only live on a street. It lives in a time of day, a payday, a text message, a person, a song, a smell, a feeling. If you don't know your triggers, they pick the time and the place. If you do, you get to pick.

Most cravings feel like they'll last forever and don't. They rise, peak, and pass, often within fifteen to thirty minutes. You don't have to win forever. You just have to outlast the next fifteen minutes. That's a strategy you can actually use tonight.

The Bible says God provides “the way of escape.” Notice it doesn't say ‘the way of no temptation.’ It says a way out. Your job is to look for the door and walk through it before your mind talks you out of it: leave the room, call the person, drink the water, go outside, put your hands on something.

A slip is not the end. It is information. It tells you what to plan for next time. Shame will tell you it means you're hopeless. It doesn't. His mercies are new every morning, and that includes tomorrow morning.

Not this (the lie)But this (the truth)
“A craving means I'm failing.”A craving means your body is remembering. It isn't a verdict.
“I just need more willpower.”You need a plan, people, and a way out. Willpower alone runs out.
“If I slip once, it's over.”Relapse is not resignation. Tell somebody and start at the next right step.

Day 4: Know Your Triggers

Tool: The 15-Minute Craving Plan

Write this when you're calm. Use it when you're not.

1. My top 3 triggers (people, places, feelings, times of day)

2. Where I am most likely to get pulled back this week

3. When a craving hits, first I will (move, water, step outside)

4. The person I call first (and their number)

5. My 15-minute rule: I wait, set the timer, then I decide again.

Do This Today
Write my top 3 triggers
Pick my “call first” person and text them so they know
Practice the 15-minute wait once with a timer

Prayer

God, You said there's a way out. Open my eyes to it when the craving comes, and let my feet move toward it before my head talks me out of it. Give me fifteen minutes, then give me fifteen more. In Jesus' name, amen.

Hard day? If a craving is loud right now: set a timer for 15 minutes, drink water, and call someone. Don't decide anything until the timer goes off.

Day 5: Break the Isolation

Ecclesiastes 4:9-10 (ESV)“Two are better than one, because they have a good reward for their toil. For if they fall, one will lift up his fellow. But woe to him who is alone when he falls and has not another to lift him up!”
James 5:16 (ESV)“Therefore, confess your sins to one another and pray for one another, that you may be healed.”

Shame says hide. God says come out. Isolation is where relapse does its best work, and it's where the enemy whispers that nobody wants to hear it, nobody would understand, you're too much, you're too far gone.

That's a lie. But it's a convincing one, because it's usually been rehearsed for years. The way you break a lie like that is not to argue with it in your head. You break it by letting a real person prove it wrong.

You need people who know the truth about you. Not everybody. Not the internet. A few. A recovery group, a sponsor, a counselor, a pastor who actually knows what to do with this, a friend who stays. Look for people who tell the truth and stay.

If church hurt you, I'm sorry. Spiritual abuse is real, and it's not God. There are churches and groups that do this well. Celebrate Recovery is one faith-based option, and AA and NA meetings are in almost every town. Try more than one. Fit matters.

Not this (the lie)But this (the truth)
“I don't want to burden anybody.”Letting people help is not a burden. It's how the body of Christ works.
“Nobody would understand.”Somebody in a meeting room tonight has been exactly where you are.
“I've been hurt by church, so I'm done.”Done with the people who hurt you, yes. Not done with God, and not done with community.

Day 6: Own It Without Drowning

Proverbs 28:13 (ESV)“Whoever conceals his transgressions will not prosper, but he who confesses and forsakes them will obtain mercy.”
Romans 8:1 (ESV)“There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus.”

Grace is not permission. You can be fully forgiven and still owe repair. That's not God being harsh. That's how real relationships and real recovery work.

Owning it is a plan, not a punishment. You name what's yours. You tell the truth. You make it right where it's safe and possible. You change the pattern. And you don't confuse guilt (I did something wrong) with shame (I am something wrong). God deals with guilt through forgiveness. He deals with shame through belonging.

Now the other half, which people rarely say: some of what you're carrying isn't yours. If someone abused you, trafficked you, betrayed you, or manipulated you, that belongs to them, not to you. Don't take responsibility for what was done to you. Put it down.

And repair never means walking back into danger. If contacting someone isn't safe, then the repair is changing your own life, and letting God carry what you can't. You don't have to reconcile with anyone who is still unsafe.

Not this (the lie)But this (the truth)
“I'm forgiven, so I don't owe anything.”Forgiven and accountable. Both.
“It's all my fault.”Not everything. Sort what's yours from what was done to you.
“Forgive means go back.”Forgiveness is release. Access is earned by safe behavior over time.

Day 7: Plant Something

John 15:5 (ESV)“I am the vine; you are the branches. Whoever abides in me and I in him, he it is that bears much fruit, for apart from me you can do nothing.”
John 8:36 (ESV)“So if the Son sets you free, you will be free indeed.”

Somebody taught you to survive. The bando, the corner, the hustle: all of it was survival. But God didn't pull you out of the pit just so you could survive. He pulled you out so you could grow.

Growing is slower than surviving, and it includes pruning. In a vineyard, you cut the branch so the vine can bear more fruit. Some of what's getting cut in your life isn't punishment. It's preparation.

For years the search was for another plug, another source, another supply. Jesus says He is the vine, and that apart from Him you can do nothing. The Source was never on the corner. You don't have to keep looking.

You will not become the woman or man God is calling you to be overnight. You become her every time you choose not to go back. Your job for the next thirty days is simple: pick a daily anchor, pick a weekly anchor, tell someone, and keep going. The next right step. Then the next.

Not this (the lie)But this (the truth)
“I've messed up too much for God to use me.”God doesn't call the qualified. He qualifies the called. And He starts with what's left.
“I need to become perfect first.”You don't. You need to stay connected to the vine.
“One relapse ruins everything.”It doesn't. Tell the truth, call your people, start at the next right step.

The 5 Lies That Keep People Bound

The enemy tells lies that sound like your own voice. Learn to spot them, and answer them with truth out loud.

The lie: “I'm too far gone.”

The truth: Nobody is beyond the reach of God. Ephesians 2 says He made us alive when we were dead. D.O.A. is His favorite starting point. (Ephesians 2:4-5)

The lie: “If they knew the truth, they'd leave.”

The truth: Some people might. The right ones won't. And God already knows and stays. (Psalm 34:18)

The lie: “One slip and it's all ruined.”

The truth: A slip is information, not a verdict. The righteous fall and rise again. Tell somebody and start at the next right step. (Proverbs 24:16)

The lie: “I'll be okay when I get [that person/that thing/that money].”

The truth: Only Jesus is the Source. People, things, and money are not the plug. They're just more supply that runs out. (John 15:5)

The lie: “This is just how I am.”

The truth: Your patterns are learned and they can be unlearned. Nobody is stuck. You can become someone new, one choice at a time. (2 Corinthians 5:17)

Boundaries & Forgiveness Are Not the Same as Access

You can forgive someone and still keep the door closed. You can love someone and still say no. Boundaries are not punishment. They are how you protect what God is rebuilding.

SituationWhat you can say
Someone pressures you to use or drink“No thanks. I'm not doing that anymore.” (No explanation needed.)
Old friends invite you to the old places“I love you, but I'm not going there. I can meet for coffee somewhere else.”
Someone wants money and you know where it goes“I can't give you cash. I can buy you groceries or drive you to a meeting.”
A person who hurt you wants back in“I forgive you, and I'm not letting you back in. Please respect that.”
Family pressures you to “just forgive and move on”“Forgiving is between me and God. Trust and access are earned, and they're not available right now.”
Someone uses Scripture to make you stay in danger“God doesn't ask me to stay somewhere I'm being harmed.”

Safety note: if the person is dangerous, do not confront them in person. Don't announce a boundary to someone who may react violently. Talk to a hotline advocate about a safe plan first (1-800-799-7233).

If I Slip: My Plan

Write this while you're steady. Then when you're not, you don't have to think. You just follow the plan.

First hour
Get somewhere safe. If I've used a lot, or feel unwell: 911
Call my person: name / number
Drink water, eat something, don't use anything else tonight
Same day
Tell one safe person the truth. Not tomorrow. Today.
Go to a meeting or call one (or SAMHSA 1-800-662-4357)
Write what happened, what triggered it, and what I'll do differently
Next 7 days
Go back to the last thing that was working (a step, a meeting, a person)
Tell my counselor, sponsor, or doctor
Restart at the next right step, not at zero

Remember: a slip is not the end of your story. Shame tells you to hide. God says come back. “There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus.” (Romans 8:1, ESV)

Medical note: if you've been sober for a while and slip, your body may not tolerate the amount you used before. Overdose risk is higher after a break. Be careful, and get help.

Prayers for Hard Moments

Real prayers, short enough to pray with shaky hands. Change the words to sound like you. God isn’t grading the grammar.

When the craving is loud

Jesus, I'm not going to win this one alone. Come stand between me and this. I'm going to drink water, move, and call somebody. Give me fifteen minutes, and then give me fifteen more. Amen.

When it's 3 a.m. and I can't sleep

Lord, You don't sleep, and You're not surprised by me. Quiet my mind. Take the things I can't fix tonight, and let me rest. Show me the one next right step in the morning. Amen.

After a slip

Father, I fell. I'm not going to hide it from You or from the people You've put in my life. I confess it, and I receive Your mercy, because Your Word says there's no condemnation for those in Christ. Help me get up and start at the next right step. Amen.

When I'm angry at God

God, I'm mad, and You already know. I don't understand why some things happened. I'm not going to pretend. But I'm still here, and I'm asking You to meet me in it. Amen.

For the person I love who is still bound

Lord, I can't save them. You can. Give me wisdom to love them without enabling them, and boundaries that protect me and them. Keep them alive today. Put people and doors and truth in their path. Amen.

For the person listening from the floor tonight

Lord, if someone is reading this from the floor tonight, be closer than the pain. Send help, send a phone number, send a person. Let them feel that they are seen and not alone. And give them the courage to take just one more step. In Jesus' name, amen.

How to make the first call

  1. Say: “I'm looking for help with (alcohol / drugs / safety). Where do I start?”
  2. Ask about cost and insurance. Many places have sliding scales or free options.
  3. Ask about medical detox if you've been using daily.
  4. Write down names and numbers as you go. Bring a person if you can.
  5. If the first call doesn't work, call another. You only need one yes.
